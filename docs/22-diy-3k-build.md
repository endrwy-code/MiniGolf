# A DIY course on about S$3k: one sheet, one module (draft, 28 Nov)

**Status:** Claude's answer to a team member's ask on 28 Nov: "Research how we can build our own course on a S$3k budget, with limited experience. Make it clean, robust, nice to look at and sturdy, on the challenging side to play. Fact-check ChatGPT's list and tweak it to what we need." Proposal, not a decision. Every price is marked **found** (a Singapore listing, with its source and the date checked, 28 Nov 2026) or **estimate**. Nothing has been bought or contacted. Working notes, sources and rejected options: `working-papers/22-diy-3k-notes.md`.

## Summary and verdict

**Verdict: S$3k works, but only just, and only as a simpler course than doc 14.** S$3k buys ten clean, sturdy holes built by volunteers from one material (12 mm plywood), with sunk cups to the doc 13 standard, if four things are free: putters, lights, a workshop and a table saw. It leaves a contingency of about S$260 (9%), which one bad plywood quote would eat, because the only Singapore price found is S$36 a sheet and the course needs about 37 sheets. **Plan on S$3.5k; S$4k is comfortable** and adds an LED glow line on every rail, paid strip-cutting and 15 bought putters. ChatGPT's list (S$1.8–3.0k) has roughly the right lines, but it undercounts plywood by about three times and putters by about three times, buys the wrong surface (turf instead of short carpet) and the wrong rails (PVC and timber), and misses transport, glue and fixings, module joints, printing, PPE and contingency.

**The system.** Every hole is built from a few identical plywood modules. One 2440 × 1220 sheet of 12 mm plywood makes one 2440 × 900 deck module: the 900 mm strip is the deck top, and the 320 mm offcut becomes its ribs. The deck stands 74 mm high, so a 110 mm drain-pipe cup sinks 61 mm anywhere, as doc 13 asks. The rails are 12 mm plywood strips screwed to the deck's edge as a fascia, so they hide the structure, stand exactly 100 mm above the carpet, and get a white cap: one profile, one colour, one height on every hole. Needle-punch event carpet is wrapped over each deck and clamped by the rails, so it can't creep and needs no tape on the venue floor. Slopes and terraces come from taller ribs, not extra decks. The repeatable parts (deck, rail, cap, cup, ramp) are built with simple jigs, so volunteers get the same result every time.

**The holes.** Ten New York holes keep doc 14's names and most of its ideas, rebuilt from straight lines: 10 Brownstone Stoops (drops), 9 Brooklyn Pizza (one-shot curling), 8 Hot Dog Cart (gate and bun funnel), 7 Brooklyn Bridge (hill with the cup on top), 6 Liberty Island (risky causeway or safe channels), 5 Central Park (double bank), 4 Gridlock (cab field), 3 The Subway (pick a tunnel), 2 Grand Central (bank round the booth), 1 Times Square (two-step climb). Curves, the loop box, plinko and the wave are dropped: they need a skilled carpenter or CNC. Par 26; each hole should take a group of 4 about 1.5–2.5 minutes.

**Top three risks.** (1) Nobody on the team can rip 250 accurate strips: book a confident volunteer with a table saw now, or pay the yard (the S$4k version). (2) Plywood costs more than S$36: get two quotes on Mon 30 Nov; every S$4 a sheet adds about S$150. (3) Volunteer hours (about 370–480) clash with Christmas production: fix the build nights now and freeze the design at the G2 prototype on Fri 4 Dec.

## 2. The build system

### 2.1 Four ways to make the rails and the base, compared

The cup decides a lot. Doc 13 needs a flush, sleeved cup 60–68 mm deep, and nothing can go into the venue floor, so every cup needs about 70 mm of structure under the surface. A floor-laid hole (route C) has to build a raised box at every cup anyway. Building the whole hole as a 74 mm plywood box costs little more, because the ribs come out of the same sheet as the top.

| Option | How it works | Cost for our course (estimate) | Looks | Plays | Skill | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Plywood rails on floor-laid carpet** | Carpet on the floor; rails of two laminated 12 mm plywood strips (24 × 100 mm) screwed to hidden 300 × 300 plates under the carpet (route C's D1); a raised plywood box at every cup | ≈ 18 sheets ≈ S$650, plus floor-safe tape ≈ S$50–100 | Good, if the carpet stays flat | The lane follows every dip in the floor; carpet on a glossy floor creeps and ripples; every hole must end on a ramp to its cup box | Low to medium | **Fallback.** About S$700 cheaper, but messier and less sturdy. Use it if plywood quotes above about S$45 a sheet or nobody can rip strips accurately |
| **(b) Low plywood deck modules (recommended)** | Each module is a 12 mm deck top on 55 mm plywood ribs ripped from the same sheet; the rails are fascia strips screwed to the deck edge; carpet wrapped over the deck and clamped by the rails | ≈ 37 sheets ≈ S$1,330 for decks, ribs, rails, caps and obstacles | Cleanest: no visible deck edge, one rail profile, no tape | Flat and shimmable; cups sink anywhere; slopes and terraces come from rib heights; carpet can't creep | Medium: one person rips strips on a table saw; volunteers assemble with jigs | **Recommended** |
| (c) Solid timber rails (planed 2"×4", 45 × 95) | Classic DIY rail, screwed down to plates or decks | Rails alone ≈ S$2,000 at retail (planed kapur 2"×4"×8 ft S$52.90, found); a timber yard may be cheaper, unquoted | Good, if straight | Good rebound | Low | **Rejected for S$3k.** Use it only if a yard quotes straight, dressed stock under about S$8 a metre. Solid timber bows and twists; ripped plywood strips are dead straight and identical |
| (d) Ready-made borders | PVC pipe, garden edging, pool noodles, foam, PVC skirting or cable trunking | ≈ S$100–300 (estimate) | Looks like a carnival or plumbing | PVC pipe has a round top that balls climb, and it rolls; garden edging is floppy and wavy; foam and noodles kill the rebound; skirting is too low; trunking is hollow and flexes | Low | **Rejected.** A bank shot needs a hard, flat, rigid face about 100 mm high (a bumper-golf patent specifies sides of 1/2-inch material, 4 inches high, so the ball strikes at or just above its widest point: US5413344) |

**Why 12 mm plywood, and only 12 mm.** It is the one Singapore price found (S$36 a sheet, 12 mm WBP, Hock Chuan Seng, 28 Nov; the page doesn't say whether GST is included). It is thick enough for a deck on ribs at 300 mm, a rail 100 mm proud, and screws. Using one thickness for everything means one order, one blade setting and no mix-ups. Commercial grade is fine for one night indoors; marine plywood (S$90 a sheet at Hardware City, found) is not needed. **Ask the yard about 12 mm film-faced (formwork) plywood for the rail sheets:** its dark phenolic faces are hard and slick, which is what a bank face wants (doc 14 M7), and they need no paint. Use it if it costs within about S$5 of plain plywood.

### 2.4 Cutting: what the yard does, what volunteers do

**Can a Singapore timber yard cut sheets for us? Yes, probably, but nobody publishes a price.** Singapore plywood sellers (Chi Han Trading, Hong Wen, Hock Chuan Seng, Chip Seng Timber) list cutting "for a fee" or "enquire". Overseas benchmarks are A$3.50 + GST per cut (Plyco, Australia) and £1 per cut after two free (Selco, UK). The course needs about 250 rip cuts, so per-cut pricing at S$2–5 (estimate) would be S$500–1,250: too much for S$3k.

**So split the cutting:**
- **Ask the yard for a per-sheet price to rip the 19 deck sheets once (900 + 320) and the 9 rail sheets into strips.** These are the cuts that show and that must be dead straight. If it is S$200 or less, take it: that is in the S$4k version.
- **A volunteer with a borrowed table saw rips the ribs.** Ribs must all be the same height (55 ± 0.5 mm), so rip them all in one session at one fence setting, and never mix batches. A circular saw with a clamped straight-edge guide works if no table saw is found, at about ±1 mm.
- **Cross-cuts** (rib lengths, rail ends, 45° corners) on a borrowed mitre saw, or with a handsaw in a mitre box.

### 2.5 Jigs and templates (so every volunteer gets the same result)

| Jig | What it is | What it guarantees |
|---|---|---|
| J1 Rib story stick | A 2440 strip marked with every rib position (0, 300, 600, 900 across; 813 and 1627 along) | Every module has ribs in the same places, so the dowels line up |
| J2 The flat floor | Build every module right way up on a flat floor, with the rails standing on the floor beside it | Rail bottom = rib bottom, so every rail stands exactly 100 above the carpet with no measuring |
| J3 Screw-spacing stick | Marks every 200 mm, with a line 30 mm up for the rail screws | Screws hit the edge rib, always in the same line |
| J4 Dowel and joint jig | A 900-wide plywood offcut with two holes at fixed positions; clamp it to an end rib and drill | Any module joins any other module |
| J5 Cup template | A 900-wide offcut with the hole's cup position marked from the rail, plus the 110 mm hole saw | Cups go where the drawing puts them, clear of ribs |
| J6 Ramp-rib template | A 300 × 170 plywood pattern for a pair of 1:5 tapered ribs | Identical ramps |
| J7 Bank-board triangle | A 45° offcut triangle | Banks at exactly 45°, so the corners play the same every time |
| J8 Paint bench | Two trestles and a long board: roll the rail and cap strips flat before assembly | Crisp, even paint with no drips on the play face |

### 2.6 Build one module, step by step

1. **Paint first.** Lay the rail and cap strips on the paint bench (J8). Sand the edges, then two coats: rails charcoal on both faces, caps white. Let them dry overnight.
2. **Ribs.** Lay the deck top face down. Mark the rib lines with J1. Glue and screw the ribs down through the top (3.5 × 30 screws at 200 mm, pilot holes centred on the rib, heads countersunk flush), doubling both edge ribs and both end ribs. The glue does the holding; the screws only clamp it while it sets. If a rib edge splits, glue a second rib alongside it.
3. **Turn it over** onto the flat floor. Check it with a 2 m straightedge: no gap over 1 mm. Plane or sand any high joint.
4. **Cup.** Clamp J5 on, drill the 110 hole, glue in the cup can (sleeve plus hardboard bottom), and sand its rim flush with the plywood.
5. **Carpet.** Cut a 970-wide strip (two strips from a 2 m roll) to length. Lay it with no wrinkles, fold it over both long edges and 20 mm down the edge ribs, and staple it there. Star-cut the cup opening and fold the flaps into the sleeve. Tape and staple the module ends.
6. **Rails.** Stand each rail on the floor against the deck edge (J2). Glue it to the lower part of the edge rib (the carpet covers only its top 20 mm) and screw it through into the rib at 200 mm with **3.5 × 40** screws (J3), so each screw bites the full 24 mm of rib. Fix the end rail the same way; the side rail laps over its end. Fill the outside screw holes. Rails taller than 231 (holes 10 and 1) get a 24 × 45 stiffener on the outside face every 600 mm, glued and screwed to the rail and the edge rib. Blocks, walls and bank boards are screwed up through the deck from below: two people tip the module on its side to fix them.
7. **Caps.** Glue and pin or screw the caps on, flush with the inside face. Round every exposed corner and edge with sandpaper.
8. **Rubber.** Glue the 3 mm rubber or EVA strip under every rib and rail.
9. **Dowels and joint holes** with J4. Label the module on its underside and on the outer rail (for example "H4-M2"), with an arrow to the next module.
10. **Test.** Roll a ball slowly along it three times: it should run straight. Stand on it: no creak and no visible dip.

**Raised parts** (steps, ramps, hill, causeway) are the same module with taller ribs under part of the top: the top is cut into pieces at the ramp's foot and crest, each joint lands on a rib, and the carpet runs over all of it in one piece. A raised module weighs about 36–40 kg: carry it with three people or a trolley.

**Install** (per hole, two people, about 30–45 minutes once practised): place the modules in order on the marked spot, slide the dowels home, screw the splice plates and the bridging caps on, check the level with a 2 m straightedge, shim under ribs with plywood offcuts if needed, set the flats and the sign, roll a ball.

### 2.7 Rules for clean lines

- **One rail, one colour, one height:** charcoal plywood rails, white caps, 100 mm above the surface on every hole. No timber, pipe or foam rails anywhere.
- **Straight lines and 45° only.** No curves: they are what made doc 14 slow and messy to build.
- **No visible deck edge.** The rails cover them.
- **No carpet seams inside a lane.** The 2 m roll gives two 970 strips; seams fall only at module joints, over a doubled rib.
- **No screw shows on a play face.** Deck screws are countersunk under the carpet; rail screws go in from outside and are filled; blocks and walls are screwed from below.
- **Four carpet colours only:** green for most lanes; charcoal "asphalt" (Gridlock, the Subway); blue "harbour" (Liberty); red "steps" (Times Square).
- **Props are flats, outside the rails or fixed across them, never loose.** One bold flat per hole beats many small props (doc 13 section 5.4).

### Each hole in more detail

Positions are in millimetres: x along the lane from the tee-end rail, z across from the left rail (as you face the cup).

**10 Brownstone Stoops.** The tee landing is the first 1220 at +100 above the deck. The ball drops 60 off a square, rounded nosing onto the stoop (+40, 1220 long), whose front nosing is cut 20° off square, then drops 40 onto the green (deck level, 2440 long). Cup at x 3750, 300 off the centreline (in a rib bay) on the side the skew pushes away from. The rails run level along the whole hole at 271, standing 100 above the landing and 200 above the green: a brownstone wall with a straight top. Players stand at the open tee end or step onto the landing (it is built to be stood on). Junior tee: on the stoop. Risk: kids climbing the terraces; the 12 mm tops on 300 mm ribs take it, but marshals discourage it.

**9 Brooklyn Pizza.** Tee lane 2440 at deck level. Module 2: a 1:5 crust ramp (300 long, +60), then the green at +60 (about 1990 long), then a 150 mm oven slot across the back at deck level, so any ball that reaches it drops 60 and can't come out. The pizza is a Ø760 circle stencilled on the green carpet with thinned acrylic (test on an offcut first: it must not raise the pile), with the cup at its centre, 1060 past the crest. **Scoring:** one shot each; balls stay until all four have putted, so knock-ons count; cup 1, ball on the pizza 2, elsewhere on the green 3, rolled back down the crust or in the oven 4. Children under about 8 get two shots and keep the better. The ace needs about 2.25 m/s from the tee; the 2-point zone spans about 2.14–2.35 m/s, so ±5%. The fastest hole on the course.

**8 Hot Dog Cart.** Tee at x 300. At x 1600 a cart flat stands across the lane, notched over the rails and screwed to them, with a 220 × 120 opening at deck level, its centre 150 left of the centreline (5 ball widths; no gap anywhere else under the flat). Beyond, two bank boards run from the rails at x 2440 to a 200-wide channel at x 3100, at about 28°; the triangles behind them are closed with plywood lids painted as the bun, so there are no pockets. The cup sits in the channel 250 from the end rail; a ball that runs too firm rebounds off the end and passes over the cup a second time. Mustard and ketchup painted. The S$4k version can restore it to 4.88 m with a longer approach.

**7 Brooklyn Bridge.** Three half modules: tee A, the hill, tee B. The hill rises 60 at 1:5 over 300, has a 620 plateau with the cup in the middle, and falls 300 to the other side. A ball needs about 1.25 m/s at the foot of the ramp to reach the cup, and an off-line ball runs over the top above about 1.39 m/s: a window of about 10%. **Rules:** each player picks an end; one ball moves at a time; a ball that runs over is played from where it stops. Doc 14's simultaneous "3-2-1" rally is an optional adults-only rule, not the default (ball clashes, small kids). Two tower flats stand outside the rails beside the hill, sandbagged.

**6 Liberty Island.** Module 1 is the blue harbour at deck level, with a causeway 300 wide on the centreline: a 1:5 entry ramp from x 1100 to 1400, then +60 to x 2440, where it joins the island. Either side of it, a 300-wide channel runs at deck level and ends in its own 1:5 ramp (x 2140–2440) up onto the island's corner. Module 2 is the island, +60, full width, with the cup at x 3200 on the centreline. **The choice:** the causeway route is straight at the cup but allows only about ±128 mm of error; a ball that falls off is in a channel and must go the long way round. The channel route is safe but lands on a corner of the island, leaving a 0.8–1 m putt. A painted Liberty flat (about 2.2 m, weighted base) stands outside the far rail with a battery LED torch.

**5 Central Park.** A Z: the tee module runs along x (0–2440), a 900 square at its end (x 2440–3340) turns the route through 90°, a second square above it turns it back, and a half module (x 3340–4560) is the green. Each outer corner has a 45° bank board across it (legs 450, 636 long, set with J7); the two inner corners are plain 90° rail corners. The straight line from the tee is blocked, so the ball must use both banks. Cup at about (4100, 450 from the green's left rail). Two borrowed bike wheels stand outside the rails as dressing ("Wheel See You Next Year"), with a painted park-bench flat.

**4 Gridlock.** Charcoal "asphalt" carpet with painted lane lines. Six cab blocks (300 × 150 × 100, closed and capped, painted yellow with an invented "CAB 1159"), in staggered rows: two against the rails with a 300 gap between them, then one in the middle with 300 either side, then a pair again, and one set at 30° as a bank block, at least 270 from the rail. Every gap is 0 or at least 250. Cup on a painted zebra crossing at x 3200. The S$4k version can go back to 4.88 m and eight cabs.

**3 The Subway.** Charcoal lane to a white-tile wall flat across the lane at x 1800, screwed to the rails, with three portals 100 high at deck level: Local left and Local centre 130 wide, Express right 90 wide. Behind the wall, a 600-long tunnel box of rail-strip channels with a lift-off lid (two dowels; it lifts in seconds) painted as a station roof: the Express runs straight at the cup; the Locals fan left and come out 0.4–0.7 m to the left of it. The spaces between channels are closed, so no ball can stop out of reach. Cup on the green half module at x 3100, in line with the Express. A miss bounces back off the wall. The channels are flat, so a weak putt can stop inside a tunnel: lift the roof and place the ball at that tunnel's exit, no penalty. Sign: doc 14's suggested twist "Stand Clear of the Closing Year" (S11), with invented line symbols.

**2 Grand Central.** Two full modules side by side, dowelled along their long edges (these two modules have rails only on their outer edges), make a 2440 × 1800 deck. Four 45° chamfer walls (legs 500, 707 long) cut the corners, with white triangle lids over the dead corners. The joint between the two modules runs down the middle (z 900), so the tee, the booth and the cup sit on a line 150 to one side of it, in a rib bay (z 750): tee spot at x 400, entered over a low tee rail; a gold octagonal booth block (about 300 across, 100 high) at x 1220; cup at x 2040. Because the line is off-centre, the two long walls give two different banks: the mirror bank off the near wall strikes its midpoint about 42° off the line, off the far wall about 52°. The team's 3D page found that a rolling ball comes off a rail steeper than a mirror would send it, so the prototype will show the true aiming point. A painted 1 m clock at 11:59 stands on a post outside the far end.

**1 Times Square (the prototype, section 5).** Red "steps" carpet. Tee lane 2440 at deck level. Module 2: a 1:5 ramp up 60 (x 2440–2740), an 800 flat, a second 1:5 ramp up to +120 (x 3540–3840), then a 1040 top green with the cup at x 4400. The rails along module 2 are 291, so their tops run level. The ace needs about 2.6 m/s from the adult tee; the junior tee on the first step needs about 1.6 m/s, inside doc 14's "1.8 m/s for a 6-year-old" limit. Too soft rolls back down one or both steps; too firm comes off the back rail and can roll back down. Two billboard flats (invented brands) outside the rails and a 2.4 m "2027" tower flat behind the end, weighted at 30 kg or more.

### Course checks

- **Ten mechanics:** drops, one-shot target, gate and funnel, hill, risk fork, double bank, obstacle field, choose a tunnel, bank round a blocker, two-step climb. Doc 13's team wishes still in: terraces (10), curling (9), cup built into a prop (8), the rally hill (7), changes of direction (5, 2), trick tunnels (3). Out: the curved guide rails, plinko and the wave.
- **Skill against chance:** skill-heavy 10, 9, 7, 6, 5, 2, 1; partly chance 4 and 3. Every hole has a junior tee mark nearer the cup, and the 5-stroke cap stays.
- **Every miss reachable:** lanes are 900 wide, channels 300; the oven slot is open to the hand; the tunnel lid lifts; no ball can rest on an 11° ramp; there are no acute pockets and no moving parts.

- **Story and blending at almost no cost:** the shared rail and colours, the pun signs, the four carpet colours by district, the flats, and "find the 11:59" (a sticker or painted clock on each hole).

**Prototype pass list (Fri 4 Dec).**
1. **Flat:** no gap over 1 mm under a 2 m straightedge; a slow ball runs straight down each module.
2. **Stand:** an adult stands and steps anywhere on the deck: no creak, no visible dip.
3. **Kick:** a firm kick at the top of a rail moves nothing and loosens no screw or cap.
4. **Joint:** a fingernail can't catch on it; a slow ball crosses it without a hop or a turn.
5. **Cup:** a ball resting at the lip stays in; a putt at about 1.2 m/s over the centre drops; a ball lifts out easily.
6. **Speed:** a DIY Stimp reading (release a ball down the same short ramp ten times, measure the roll-out, work out the drag), compared with the 0.6 m/s² assumed here.
7. **Steps:** at least 70% of adults and 50% of kids get up both steps within two putts; no ball hops at a ramp crest.
8. **Pace:** a group of 4 finishes in 2.5 minutes or less.
9. **Carry:** two people carry a module 20 m comfortably, and two modules stack rail on rail without touching the carpet.

| **The surface is faster than assumed** | Ramp and pizza windows shift | Measure at G2; adjust the rises (60 to 40) or move cups before building the rest |
| **Small kids and firm putts** | Hole 1's ace needs about 2.6 m/s | Junior tees on every hole; after two roll-backs, place the ball at the top of the step for +1 |
| **Accessibility** | Decks and rails exclude wheelchair users from stepping in | Low tee rails let a seated player putt from the tee end on the straight holes; a marshal places the ball on request. No money for access ramps |
| **Cables can't run under the holes** | The cups stand on the floor | Production routes floor cables round the course (as for doc 03's decks) |
| **Not enough putters to borrow** | S$3k assumes about 50 borrowed | Start the loan list on Mon 30 Nov (members, other ministries, partner churches, a golf club). Each 10 bought costs about S$130, from the contingency |
| **Budget creep** | S$3k has about S$260 of slack | One person approves spending; the contingency is only for listed risks; cut in this order: hand-painted signs, one flat fewer, holes 10 and 9 to 3.66 m |

## 8. Adversarial pass

Before finishing, Claude re-read this doc as a sceptical carpenter and as a sceptical mini golf player, listed what would go wrong, and changed the doc. What was found, and what was done:

**As a carpenter**

| Problem found | Fix made |
|---|---|
| A 3.5 × 30 screw through a 12 mm rail and 4 mm of carpet bites only about 14 mm into the edge rib, so a kicked rail could pull loose | Rail screws are now 3.5 × 40 into a doubled (24 mm) edge rib; the rail is glued to the part of the rib below the carpet (section 2.6, step 6) |
| Screwing a 12 mm top into a 12 mm rib edge can split the plies | Pilot holes centred; glue does the holding; a split rib gets a second rib glued alongside (step 2) |
| The 271 and 291 rails on holes 10 and 1 stand 200–220 mm above the lower deck and would flex when kicked | A 24 × 45 stiffener every 600 mm on the outside face (step 6) |
| A rib under the centreline would block a cup there; hole 10's cup sat on a rib line; hole 2's cup sat on the joint between its two modules | Ribs at 0, 300, 600 and 900 keep the centreline free; hole 10's cup moved to 300 off centre; hole 2's tee, booth and cup moved 150 off the joint, which also gives two different banks |
| Blocks and walls "screwed from below" can't be reached once a module sits on the floor | They are fixed while the module is tipped on its side (step 6) |
| A sharp crest on a 1:5 ramp can make a fast ball hop | Kinks eased; a 150 mm easing at 1:10 is the fallback if the prototype shows hopping (ramp row, section 2.3; pass list item 7) |
| Raised modules weigh more than the 32 kg plain module | Noted at about 36–40 kg: three people or a trolley |
| Per-cut prices at a yard could cost more than the plywood | Split the cutting: the yard rips only the visible strips (S$4k version); the ribs are ripped by the lead builder at one fence setting |

**As a player**

| Problem found | Fix made |
|---|---|
| Hole 9's one-shot rule is harsh on small children | Under-8s get two shots and keep the better |
| Hole 3's flat channels let a weak putt stop under the lid | Rule: lift the roof, place the ball at that tunnel's exit, no penalty |
| Could a ball stop halfway up a ramp, out of reach of a fair putt? | No: at 11° a ball rolls back by itself (it needs about 5° with 0.6 m/s² drag). Added to the ramp row |
| Hole 1's ace needs about 2.6 m/s, too firm for small children | Junior tee on the first step (about 1.6 m/s), and the "+1, place it at the top" rule after two roll-backs |
| Ten 900 mm straight lanes could feel samey | Two holes change direction (5, 2), and each hole has one different idea you can read from the tee. Accepted as the price of clean lines and a S$3k budget |
| Gridlock and the Subway are partly luck | Kept as the two equalisers between kids and adults; seven of the ten holes reward skill |

**Not fixed, and why**

- **Every speed and window here assumes doc 14's 0.6 m/s² carpet drag.** Needle-punch on plywood may be faster. The G2 Stimp reading settles it, and rises or cup positions change before the other nine holes are built.
- **The plywood price rests on one listing.** Until two quotes are in, the S$3k total is a target, not a plan.
- **Rebound angles off painted plywood are untested.** Hole 2 and hole 5 depend on them; the prototype week should include one bank board on the bench.
- **The fire position is unknown** until the venue answers.
