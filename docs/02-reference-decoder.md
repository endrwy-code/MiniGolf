# Reference decoder: what's in the photos, and what to copy

Files: `reference/user-refs/`, `reference/video-frames/`, `reference/inspiration-backyard-minigolf.jpg`.

For each reference, this doc asks: what is it really, what makes it look good, and what would it take to get that effect for one night in our auditorium?

---

## Photo 1: outdoor pop-up with themed platforms
`reference/user-refs/01-outdoor-popup-themed-platforms.webp`

**What it is.** A touring branded pop-up on a city plaza at night. A "Brave" hole sign suggests the Pixar Putt tour. Each hole is a **self-contained raised platform**, about 15–25 cm high, with a timber or painted fascia. The surface is turf with **graphic inlays** (stripes, shapes, coloured zones). There are props (cut-outs, sculptures, a target board), a hole-number totem with a big number, and lighting from truss towers and umbrellas.

**Why it looks good**
- Every hole is a clean, bounded "island" with a crisp edge.
- Bold flat colour on the playing surface, not just green.
- Lots of vertical elements (totems, props) add height and depth to a flat floor.
- Overhead lighting makes it glow at night.

**What to copy:** the platform method, coloured turf inlays, number totems and vertical props.
**What not to copy:** the characters and IP (don't use them), truss towers (the auditorium already has rigging and lighting), and outdoor weatherproofing.
**One-night cost to replicate:** low to medium. This is standard event set-building.

## Photo 2: "PUNK BALL" hero hole (indoor)
`reference/user-refs/02-indoor-punk-ball-hero-hole.webp`

**What it is.** One factory-made hole from an indoor entertainment centre, in the style of the Chinese family-entertainment-centre suppliers. It has a quarter-pipe ramp that sends the ball up and back, a **pin field** of short posts and red blocks like a pinball table, a maze disc on the backboard, cone bumpers, and black-and-white graffiti wraps on the sides. A **mesh fence** stops the ball leaving the hole, and there's an LED marquee sign. Materials look like steel frame, fibreglass or MDF surfaces, and printed vinyl wraps.

**Why it looks good**
- A strong single idea (pinball meets skate park), carried through every surface.
- Height: the backboard makes it read from across a room.
- It's enclosed, so the ball can't escape. That's a hidden safety and flow win.

**What to copy:** the pin-field mechanic (PVC pipe or dowel pins in a plywood deck: cheap, fun and fast), the ball-containment fence on any ramp hole, graphic side wraps (printed vinyl on plywood), and a big lit sign.
**What not to copy:** the steel quarter-pipe. It's expensive to fabricate and has a long lead time. Make a shallow plywood curve, or skip it.
**One-night cost to replicate:** medium for one hero hole. **Use this level of finish for one or two holes, not all ten.**

## Photo 3: LED-glow indoor course (Puttshack-style)
`reference/user-refs/03-indoor-led-edge-glow.webp`

**What it is.** A permanent indoor tech venue. It has **black speckled carpet turf**, **LED strip hidden under an overhanging lip** so each hole floats on light, glowing arrows on the surface, lit cup rings, table-height drink stands, festoon bulbs overhead, and screens with player names, which point to ball-tracking scoring tech.

**Why it looks good**
- The glow is *indirect*: you see the light on the floor, not the LED strip itself.
- The colour gradient runs around the perimeter, and the room is dark.
- The playing surface is dark, so a bright ball pops.

**What to copy (this is the cheapest "wow" in the whole set):**
- LED strip under a 3–5 cm overhanging top rail on each platform: roughly 12–20 m of strip per hole.
- Dark turf or needle-punch carpet as the playing surface.
- A lit cup ring (a small LED ring or a battery puck light under a translucent cup).
- UV-reactive balls and paint, plus a few UV floods.
- **A safety bonus:** a glowing edge marks the platform edge, the main trip hazard in a dark room.

**What not to copy:** ball-tracking tech and screens. They're very expensive, need integration work, and add nothing on one night. Use paper scorecards, or no scoring.
**One-night cost to replicate:** low. The catch is **power**: give the course lighting its own circuit, never ND18 or ND21.

## Video: Walker Art Center "Skyline" mini golf
`reference/video-frames/walker-skyline-contact-sheet-*.jpg`

**What it is.** A seasonal, artist-designed course on a museum roof deck. **This is the closest model to what we're doing:** temporary, modular, on a hard floor that must not be damaged, with every hole a different idea.

**What the frames show**
- Every hole is a **low platform**, about 10–15 cm, with **white painted borders**. Most are plywood and paint, with turf only on the putting area.
- Holes have **unusual outlines** (a star, a city map, a dream-catcher of petal-shaped lanes) instead of rectangles. The shape *is* the theme.
- A **sign per hole** on a weighted round base gives the hole number, name, designer and par.
- There are simple, big props: a hot dog with giant ketchup and mustard bottles ("Let's Be Frank"), a coloured cone, stairs and a ramp.
- There are painted (non-turf) surfaces: blue water, red-and-white stripes, a dotted ramp with pegs.
- There's lots of space between holes, so people can walk and wait without crowding.

**What to copy:** nearly everything. The platform build, borders, signage, painted surfaces, shaped outlines, and one strong idea per hole.
**One-night cost to replicate:** low to medium. This is the hybrid local build in practice.

## Backyard windmill course (original brief)
`reference/inspiration-backyard-minigolf.jpg`

**What it is.** An outdoor DIY course: a blue-and-yellow windmill, a red barn tunnel with checkered sails, a white water wheel, timber edging, stone borders, a red-painted path and real grass.

**What to keep:** the **set-piece ideas** (windmill, barn tunnel, water wheel) and the **timber rail borders**, which give a good rebound.
**What to change for indoors at night:** grass, brick and stone don't fit an auditorium floor. If the team picks the Neon Countdown direction, rebuild the windmill and barn as glow or UV-painted props.

## Putting it together: one art direction

| Element | Neon Countdown (recommended) | Bright Carnival (alternative) |
|---|---|---|
| Playing surface | Charcoal or black turf, accent colours on inlays | Green turf with colour inlays |
| Edges | White or black rail with LED strip under the lip | Painted timber rail |
| Lighting | Low room light, LED edges, UV floods | General wash, festoon bulbs |
| Hero props | Neon-style windmill, barn tunnel, pinball, giant clock | Classic windmill, barn and wheel in bright colours |
| Signage | Glowing number totems, 10 down to 1 | Printed totems, 10 down to 1 |
| Best if | The room is dark during the party | The room is fully lit |

**The question that decides it:** how will the auditorium be lit while golf is running? Ask production.
