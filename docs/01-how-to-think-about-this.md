# How to think about this project

Written 16 Nov 2026, 45 days before the event. This is the "orient me" document: the logic, the uncomfortable truths and the order to decide things in. Read it before the supplier, hole and budget docs.

Assumption: the team is in Singapore. Prices are in S$ unless marked. Correct this if it's wrong.

## 1. Start from what cannot move

Most planning mistakes come from deciding the fun parts (suppliers, props, colours) before the fixed parts. Fix these first, in this order:

| Fixed thing | What we know | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| **Date** | Thu 31 Dec 2026. Today is Mon 16 Nov: **45 days**. | Anything with a long lead time (custom factory orders, sea freight) is on the critical path from day one. |
| **Install window** | Unknown. If weekend services on Sat 26 and Sun 27 Dec use the auditorium, the real window is **Mon 28 to Wed 30 Dec: about 3 days.** | Holes must arrive pre-built, pre-tested and plug-and-play. Nothing gets built in the room. |
| **Venue** | Auditorium, ≈46 × 11.6 m per open half. Cables cross the floor and two circuits are already over 100%. | Raised bases or rerouted cables. Any lighting on the course needs its own power, not ND18 or ND21. |
| **Safety** | Indoors, at night, crowded, probably dark with show lighting. | Fire-rated materials, no new trip edges, no balls flying out into the crowd. |
| **Budget** | **Unknown. This is the biggest gap.** | Without a number, every supplier conversation is guesswork. Get a ceiling this week. |
| **Crowd** | Unknown. | It decides how many holes, how fast they must play, and whether we need timed slots. |

**If you only do one thing this week: get a budget ceiling and an expected crowd number.** Every other decision depends on them.

## 2. Truths to align on

These correct ideas that are easy to drift into when you're looking at inspiration photos.

### Truth 1: The references are not one look. Pick one.

The five references are five different things:

| Reference | What it really is | Typical budget class |
|---|---|---|
| Outdoor pop-up with themed platforms (photo 1) | A touring branded pop-up (a "Brave" hole sign suggests the Pixar Putt tour). Event-production build: raised platforms, printed graphics, props and truss lighting. | High, but the *method* is cheap |
| "PUNK BALL" hole (photo 2) | One factory-made hero hole from an indoor entertainment centre: steel and fibreglass, printed wraps, a mesh fence. | High per hole |
| LED-glow indoor course (photo 3) | A permanent tech venue in the Puttshack style. Player-name screens mean ball-tracking tech. | Very high; the tech is the expensive part |
| Walker Art Center "Skyline" (video) | Artist-designed holes on low plywood platforms on a roof deck. Temporary, seasonal and modular. | **Low to medium. This is our build method.** |
| Backyard windmill course (brief) | A DIY outdoor build with real grass and brick. | Low, but it's a daylight look |

Mixing all five gives visual noise. Choose **one art direction** and borrow only *methods* from the rest. See `02-reference-decoder.md`.

> **Update 18 Nov:** the team now points to the Pixar Putt pop-up (bright colour, lit from above) and wants the holes to read as one "city". The recommendation has moved to **"Bright City"**: see [`09-city-concept.md`](09-city-concept.md). The Neon reasoning below is kept for the record.

**Earlier recommendation (16 Nov): a "Neon Countdown" look.** Use dark turf, glowing LED edges, UV-reactive paint and big numbers 10 to 1, with the windmill, barn and ramp turned into neon-style hero props. Four reasons:
1. The room will be dark with show lighting at a night party. The backyard daylight look will read flat there, and glow is made for it.
2. Glow is cheap. LED strip under a lip and UV paint cost little compared with fibreglass sculpture.
3. Darkness forgives imperfect carpentry. Daylight shows every seam.
4. It photographs well on phones at night, and photos are half the point of a party activity.

### Truth 2: For one night, you're not buying durability. You're buying four things.

What a player actually notices on the night:
1. **The ball rolls true.** The base is level, the turf is consistent and the borders bounce back cleanly.
2. **It looks great under the room's lighting.**
3. **There's a story.** The countdown runs 10 to 1, and each hole has a moment.
4. **No long wait.** The flow works.

Nobody notices whether a hole would survive five years of daily use. Commercial manufacturers mostly sell durability: fibreglass, steel and powder-coated aluminium. So for a one-off, paying for that is paying for something nobody will see, *unless the church will reuse the course* (Truth 4).

### Truth 3: The date decides the supplier, not the catalogue.

Work backwards from 28 Dec for a custom factory order from China:

```
Design sign-off + deposit ........ 3–7 days    → ~23 Nov
Production ........................ 15–30+ days → ~10–23 Dec
Sea freight to SG + customs ....... 7–14 days   → ~17 Dec – 6 Jan
Install window .................... 28–30 Dec
```

The overlap leaves **zero slack**, during the pre-Christmas freight peak and the pre-Chinese New Year factory rush (CNY 2027 is 6 Feb). Air freight saves about a week but costs a lot for bulky items, because air freight charges by volume, not weight. One late container, one wrong colour or one customs query, and there's no course.

So a full factory order is only realistic if the supplier has **ready-made stock** of standard holes that ships within days, and you have accepted that you get their designs, not ours. The supplier docs test this directly.

### Truth 4: The real fork is reuse.

> **Resolved 17 Nov:** one night only, movable, no reuse. So: build locally, and spend on the look and the flow. See `08-budget-model.md`.

| If the course is… | Then… |
|---|---|
| **Truly one night** | Build locally (hybrid): buy turf, and a local set builder makes everything else. Spend on the look and the flow. |
| **Likely reused** (youth events, camps, next year's countdown) | It becomes an *asset* decision. Still build locally for 31 Dec, then buy a proper modular course **for 2027 with a normal lead time** and do the factory due diligence properly. |

Don't let time pressure push you into buying a permanent asset in a rush. That's how you overpay *and* take delivery risk.

### Truth 5: Everything you love in the photos can be made locally, for one night.

| What makes the references look good | How to get it locally |
|---|---|
| Raised platforms with clean, coloured edges | Plywood on battens, 10–15 cm high, painted fascia (method from photo 1 and the Walker course) |
| Glow (photo 3) | LED strip under an overhanging lip, plus UV floods and UV-reactive paint |
| Graphics on the playing surface | Coloured turf inlays, or painted or printed vinyl on a hard surface |
| Hero props (windmill, barn, ramp) | Plywood or hard-coated foam, the core skill of event set builders |
| Big hole-number totems | Printed boards on weighted stands, like the Walker course's signs |
| The pin-field "pinball" feel (photo 2) | A grid of PVC pipe or dowel pins on a plywood deck |

Things you **can't** cheaply copy, and shouldn't try to: ball-tracking tech and screens, motorised moving parts that must run for hours without failing, and branded characters. **Don't use Pixar or Disney characters.** That's someone else's intellectual property. Borrow the method, not the characters.

### Truth 6: The bottleneck is throughput, not holes.

Rough maths, to replace with your crowd number:
- A group of 4 takes about 2–2.5 minutes per hole, so a round of 10 holes takes about 20–25 minutes.
- With one group per hole, about 40 people play at once.
- That gives **about 100–120 players per hour.** Over a 2.5-hour window, that's about 250–300 players.

If the party has 1,500 people, most of them won't play unless you design for it. The levers are: a **5-stroke cap** per hole, shorter holes, simple tee-off rules, timed slots or a queue app, and a final hole that keeps the ball. See `07-operations-and-safety.md`.

### Truth 7: The overloaded circuits are a party problem, not a golf problem.

ND18 is at 101% and ND21 at 106% on the cable plan. Raise this with whoever owns power today, whatever happens with the golf. Any course lighting (LED strip, UV floods) needs its own budgeted circuit.

## 3. The decision sequence

Decide in this order. Each step narrows the next.

1. **Budget ceiling and crowd estimate.** This week.
2. **Reuse or one-off?** This decides buy versus build.
3. **Install window confirmed** with the venue and production. This decides how plug-and-play the modules must be.
4. **Art direction:** one look, signed off. Recommended since 18 Nov: Bright City (`09-city-concept.md`).
5. **Build route,** chosen by date and quotes (see `04-sourcing.md`):
   - (a) Hybrid local build, recommended
   - (b) Rent a portable course, as a price ceiling and fallback
   - (c) Factory order, only with ready stock and only if reuse is real
6. **Hole designs frozen** (see `03-hole-spec-sheet.md`) by about 23 Nov.
7. **Prototype one hole** by about 30 Nov and test-play it: level, roll speed, border rebound, glow.
8. **Build everything,** then **dry-run assemble** at the workshop by 18 Dec.
9. **Ops plan:** queue, staff, putters, balls, scoring, reset.
10. **Install** on 28–30 Dec, with a safety walk-through before doors.

## 4. How to judge any supplier (including Huadong Play)

Score every option on these, in this order:

1. **Can it be installed and test-played by 30 Dec?** Ask for a dated plan. If they can't give one, it fails.
2. **Does it fit our art direction,** or are we bending our idea to their catalogue?
3. **Total landed cost,** not unit price: product, freight, duties and GST, local delivery, install labour, power and lighting, storage or disposal afterwards.
4. **Evidence they've delivered before:** real project photos (not renders), references you can call, verified trade records.
5. **Safety paperwork:** fire-rating certificates for turf, foam and wraps.
6. **What happens to it on 1 Jan:** storage, resale, reuse, disposal.

## 5. What "good" looks like on the night (success criteria)

Proposed, for the team to adjust:
- ≥ X players (set X from the crowd number); average wait under 15 minutes.
- Every hole is playable in 5 strokes or fewer by a first-timer.
- Zero injuries and zero trips at platform edges or cable crossings.
- Every hole is lit well enough to see the ball, and looks great in phone photos.
- Install finished and test-played by 6 pm on 30 Dec.
