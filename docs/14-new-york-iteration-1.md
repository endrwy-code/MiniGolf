# New York Countdown, iteration 1: ten holes after Kulnari (draft, 27 Nov)

**Status:** proposal by Claude for the team to react to. It is iteration 1 of the New York holes after the team's Kulnari visit (`13-kulnari-benchmark.md`), built from two submitted designs (A and B) and put through an adversarial review whose must-fix items are folded into the holes below (section 8). Both designs and the full review are kept in `working-papers/`. Recommended, not decided. Nothing here is measured: every size, speed, time and price is an estimate or design target until the Fri 4 Dec prototype and the Fri 18 Dec dry run.

## In one minute

- **Concept:** "New York Countdown: Brooklyn to Broadway". Ten holes, ten mechanics, two 5-hole districts looping from the Tee Booth. Downtown (10–6) goes **up and down**: stoops, pizza, hot-dog cart, Brooklyn Bridge, Liberty's wave. Midtown (5–1) goes **round**: Central Park, Fifth Avenue, the subway, Grand Central, Times Square.
- **Against the current ten (doc 10):** 3 holes upgraded in place (6, 2, 1), Gridlock kept and moved to 4, Hot Dog and Subway upgraded and moved (8, 3), 4 new holes (10, 9, 7, 5). Big Apple, Rink and Broadway/Rooftop retire. Every slot keeps its LED colour; 7 of 10 signs survive.
- **The team's wishes are in:** mismatched terraces, curling, cups built into props, the rally hill, a Kulnari-style wave, guiding curves, "left tube in, right tube out", plinko.
- **Pace:** with the review's pessimistic times, most holes run 2.0–2.5 min per group of 4; hole 5 is borderline at up to 2.7. About 75–80 players an hour in practice, the bottom of the 77–90 target. Plan one-district rounds at peak from the start.
- **Cost and labour:** about S$12.0–13.5k after route C's cuts and loans (current plan about S$10.9k). About 19 carpenter-days against route C's 12, so a second carpenter for 6–8 days plus CNC cutting, not a 3-day mate.
- **Prototype:** hole 3, The Subway, built Mon 30 Nov – Wed 2 Dec, test-played Fri 4 Dec (G2). Its loop floor was redrawn after the review; the drawing must be finished this weekend.
- **Risk, and how it is contained:** five holes (3, 2, 6, 7, 1) rest on physics that only a test can prove. Each has a full-size rig on Thu 3 Dec and a named fallback that keeps its footprint, and they are frozen on Sat 5 Dec. The other five (10, 9, 8, 5, 4) are lower-risk box, rail and arc carpentry that can be released as soon as the set is approved.
- **Decisions needed:** approve the set (or pick fallback rungs); accept about S$12–13.5k and book the second carpenter and CNC now; approve the format changes; release hole 3's drawing and materials for Mon 30 Nov.

## The two districts, and rooms in a big hall

**Blending.** Holes never intersect; they share scenery.

- 10 and 9 share one 4.8 × 2.4 m brownstone façade: stoops behind 10, the pizzeria shopfront behind 9's tee, and a wall against the runway. **Bracing (M8):** it has only the minimum 1.75 m to the runway, so it can't be braced from behind. Steel angle outriggers run from its base rail under hole 9's and hole 10's frames, so the holes are its ballast, with one 2 × 15 kg L-jack in the 2.2 m gap on the hole side. The alternative is two hinged panels in a shallow zig-zag. All inside z 9.9; the runway-facing back is painted or draped.

- Everywhere: the same rail, font and green street-blade totems, zebra crossings from each cup to the next tee, and a stripe of the neighbour's colour on each facing prop.

### 10 Brownstone Stoops

**Plays.** Stand on the floor beside the open tee side and putt along the top landing. The ball drops 65 mm onto a stoop that sags toward the façade, 20 mm over a lip skewed 20°, then 40 mm onto the green, and runs at the cup in a painted cellar hatch. A soft putt stalls on a terrace and is played on from there. The unequal drops are the team's "slopes that don't match".

**Mechanism.** Lane 0.95 m; four boxes: tee landing +200 mm (1.0 m), T2 +135 (1.2 m, 1.5° cross-fall, about 0.18 m/s², enough to bend a slow ball), T3 +115 (1.0 m, skewed lip), green +75 (1.9 m, cup 1.2 m past the last lip, 0.2 m off centre away from the sag). Lips rounded 15–20 mm with PVC angle nosing. About 1.1 m/s reaches the first lip; the review puts the ace at about 2.4–2.5 m/s, since a drop adds no forward speed (S2). Junior tee on T2.

**Build.** Four framed boxes (2 sheets), one straight stepped rail frame, back corners filled. Carpenter 1.25 days (1.0). Volunteers about 2 days: brownstone paint, iron-railing silhouettes, the shared façade with lit windows and a fire escape, green LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** A short ball rests on a terrace within 0.5 m of a rail; nothing rolls back up a step. Risks: chipped lips; players stepping onto terraces (S2); the sag read as a build fault (paint a crack); the +200 tee for small kids. Shares the façade with 9. **11:59:** a window clock.

### 9 Brooklyn Pizza

**Plays.** One shot each, curling style: putt down the lane, up the "crust" kerb and onto a 1.6 m pizza. The ball's centre scores: centre pepperoni cup 1, inner circle 2, on the pizza 3, anywhere else 4. Balls stay until all four have putted, so knock-ons count. The youngest player calls close ones.

**Mechanism.** Lane 2.6 m, 0.95 m wide, flaring to 1.75 m. Crust: a full-width 1:5 bevel rising 75 mm, needing about 1.2 m/s at its foot with drag (M6; the design said 1.0–1.1), so dead-weight balls roll back and score 4. House: a +75 deck levelled to ±2 mm with the pizza, eight Ø150 pepperoni, the centre one as the flush cup, and a Ø0.60 m dashed inner circle. Centre 3.8 m from the tee; ace about 2.3 m/s; the 2-point window is about ±4% of speed.

**The oven (M6).** As designed, a smash bounced off the foam backstop back onto the pizza for a 3, so hitting hard paid. The run-out and foam are replaced by a full-width **oven gutter** behind the crust ring: 120 mm wide, 45 mm deep, its floor falling 3° to a 250 mm open pocket at one rail for hand pick-up. Any ball in the oven scores 4 and can't come back.

**Build.** Deck (2 sheets), bevel, flare walls, gutter and pocket. Carpenter 0.75 day (0.6). Volunteers about 1.5 days: the pizza (inlay, or painted per S12), a 1.2 m slice sign, the shopfront, "1 SHOT · CURLING" on the totem, orange LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Short rolls back; long drops into the oven; nothing to clear. Risks: needle-punch speed (strip on 3 Dec), seams (S12), floor flatness, kids asking for another go. The brownstone's ground floor. **11:59:** the menu board.

### 8 Hot Dog Cart

**Plays.** Putt under the cart's arch between the ketchup and mustard bottles, up a short kerb and into an open giant bun whose curved faces steer the ball along a mustard squiggle to the cup near its closed end. Too soft rolls back off the kerb; too hard rebounds off the rounded end. The friendly ace hole and the food photo.

**Mechanism.** Lane 1.3 m, floor-laid. Arch 430 mm high, full width; counter 0.6 m deep. 1:5 kerb to +75 at 2.7 m (about 1.2 m/s at its foot with drag, S1). Bun halves 1.3 m long, 150 mm high, mouth 0.90 m narrowing to a 0.40 m channel (faces about 13°), closed end R 0.20 m, cup 0.30 m from the end. Halves fill to the rails (gap 0); inner faces 5 mm EVA, mouth noses PVC angle. Ace about 2.2–2.4 m/s. Junior tee 1.0 m past the cart.

**Build.** Bun boxes with a bent 3 mm hardboard skin, counter and arch flat. Carpenter 1.0 day (0.75). Volunteers about 1.5 days: golden bun, Ø180 bottles touching the rails, fries, umbrella on a post outside the rail with a sandbag, red LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Short rolls back; wide stays in the lane; under the counter is at most 0.3 m in. Risks: mouth corners (PVC angle, and S3); the cup must be dead flush; aces over about 40% move the cup 0.1 m off axis. The street corner; a "FERRY →" sign points on. **11:59:** "Open till 11:59".

### 7 Brooklyn Bridge

**Plays.** One pair at each end of a narrow bridge with a hump and the cup on top. On "3-2-1" both putt at once. Too soft rolls back; too hard crosses to the other borough. Then the second pair. Groups with small kids take turns from the ends.

**Mechanism (M5).** Lane 5.0 m with D-shaped end rails. A **0.60 m** plateau at +150 mm with the cup in the middle (was 0.45), 12° ramps with R 600 transitions (about 0.95 m each), approaches about 1.25 m. Rails **converge from 0.9 to 0.50 m over each ramp** ("the roadway narrows between the towers"), so the 10 mm rubber catch ring (Ø190) covers about 40% of the line. Ramps cross-fall **3°** toward the centre (was 2°). With slope drag a ball needs about 1.80 m/s at the foot to crest and stops on top up to about 1.99 m/s, about 2.2–2.35 m/s from the tee; over about 0.85 m/s at the crest it runs over. **Rule:** a ball that crosses is played by its owner from the end it reached, after the other player. Junior tees 0.6 m nearer.

**Build.** Five graded ribs, a 2 × 6 mm flexi skin, an 18 mm plateau for the cup sleeve, tapered rails; two tower flats on posts outside the rails, sandbagged, festoon "cables" at least 2.4 m up. Carpenter 1.0 day (0.75). Volunteers about 1.5 days.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Every miss returns by gravity to an end. Risks: smashes ("no backswing above the knee"); clashes upsetting kids; floor level under the cross-fall; tower openings at head height (S4). The towers frame Liberty's torch. **11:59:** a bridge lamp clock.

### 6 Liberty Island

**Plays.** Putt along the blue harbour at a breaking wave in the corner. The ball rides round it 90° toward Liberty Island; with pace it climbs the shore and runs at the cup in the star-shaped pedestal. Too soft slides back to the wave foot; too hard lands deep.

**Mechanism (M4).** The review showed the drawn flat 45° face fails: a ball spends about 0.75 s up a R 450 wave at any speed, travels 1.0–1.6 m along a 1.3 m face, hits the closed cheek and comes back down leg A. The baseline is now a **conical banked corner**: the face curved in plan at about R 1.0 m on tapered radial ribs, skinned in 2 × 4 mm flexi-ply (a cone is developable, so ply wraps it), 400 mm high with the 50 mm curl lip and cheeks closed to the rails. The ball rides round at any speed. Leg A: 3.0 × 0.9 m blue carpet. Leg B: a 2.2 × 1.4 m +75 island with a 1:5 shore (about 1.2 m/s at its foot with drag), cup 1.2 m past the crest in a painted star. Check the corner's footprint in the layout rerun.

**Test (M4).** The one-rib rig can't show a cheek strike. Replace it with a full-width section, real cheeks and a 3 m run-up: 20 putts at each of 1.5, 2 and 3 m/s; pass is at least 70% toward the island. Decide by Sat 5 Dec. Fallbacks: the full 1.84 m diagonal face at R 300/300 mm (partial), then a flat 45° chamfer bank with a painted wave cut-out behind.

**Build.** Ribs and skin, curl nosing, foot seam 1 mm or less; island deck; pedestal of at least 30 kg outside the rail. Carpenter 2.0 days (1.5). Volunteers about 2 days: the 2.6 m statue with a battery flame, wave paint, harbour skyline.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Weak balls roll back to the wave foot within 0.6 m of a rail. Risks: kids treating the wave as a skate ramp (S5); pace (lever: cup 0.3 m nearer the crest). The torch is Downtown's weenie. **11:59:** Liberty's tablet, XI:LIX. Draw her from the original 1886 statue, not the Las Vegas replica.

### 5 Central Park Loop

**Plays.** Midtown's teaching hole. A park path zig-zags twice; each curved outer rail carries a well-paced ball round, under a stone arch and up a little lawn to the cup in a fountain. Sign line: "the rail is your friend".

**Mechanism.** Path 0.9 m; legs 1.8, 1.2 and 2.2 m. Outer corners curved R 0.9 m, inner corners rounded and filled. Last 1.2 m a +75 green with a 1:5 bevel; cup 0.5 m past the crest. The design assumed 10–15% speed loss per corner (ace about 2.9–3.0 m/s); the review expects 25–30%, which makes an ace doubtful (S6). Junior tee at leg 2.

**Build.** Corners of five stacked 18 mm ply arcs, the top one forming the cap, screwed to plates every 300 mm: no spring-back, no free ends (K03's failure). EVA face in the design; the review prefers a hard face (S6). Carpenter 1.0 day (0.75). Volunteers about 1.5 days: arcs, two borrowed Christmas trees with fairy lights, the existing bike wheels outside the rails, benches, lamp posts, arch, fountain, blue LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** A soft ball stops within 0.45 m of a rail; a hard one rides the curve. Risks: scrub (measure on rig (e); move the bevel if the loss exceeds 20%); tree bases breaking clearances (S6). Carries the old Bike Lane's wheels and pun. **11:59:** a park clock.

### 4 Gridlock

**Plays.** Putt through a jam of eight cabs on Fifth Avenue; bold players thread one of two avenues, and two angled cabs bank toward a manhole-cover cup on a raised zebra crossing. The kids-against-adults equaliser.

**Mechanism.** Lane 5.0 × 1.8 m charcoal needle-punch; cabs 360 × 170 × 140 mm in two staggered rows of 3 (avenues about 0.30–0.35 m) plus two at 30°. Every gap at least 0.27 m or closed. Last 1.3 m a +75 green; cup 0.5 m past the crest. Junior tee 1.5 m in.

**Build.** As in the current pack, re-jigged. Carpenter 0.5 day (0.5). Volunteers about 1 day: yellow cabs, invented "CAB 1159", crosswalk, cones painted on the rails, purple LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Every resting spot is within 0.9 m of a rail. Risks: acute V pockets by the angled cabs (S7); chaos strokes (lever: open an avenue). The kiosk of hole 3 stands on its sidewalk. **11:59:** a cab meter.

### 3 The Subway (the prototype)

**Plays.** Putt up the station steps. Funnel rails steer the ball into one of three tiled portals (left Express, centre Local, right Shuttle). It vanishes, races round a hairpin under a clear lid and shoots out on the opposite side heading back toward you, with the Express exit on the cup line. Walk round and putt out.

**Mechanism, redrawn (M1, M2).** A single ply wedge could only fall across the loop box, so every hairpin had zero pull at its entry and exit and a dead-slow ball would stall; a flat 0.8 m platform also stopped just-cresting balls and sent nose bounces back down the ramp. Now:

- **Platform** 0.5 m, falling 1:12 toward the portals: any ball that crests reaches a portal, and a nose bounce returns into a channel.
- **Portals** three × 110 mm, 30 mm PVC-faced round noses, funnel 1.0 → 0.42 m.
- **Lead-ins** 1:4 at each threshold, dropping at least 25 mm in the first 100 mm (about 37 and 50 mm for the middle and inner portals, because the loop floor slopes), so every loop starts at 0.3 m/s or more.
- **Loop floor** a plane falling across the box at 5–6°; hairpin radii 0.30, 0.44, 0.58 m; walls of three stacked 18 mm arcs; 4 mm polycarbonate lid, 60 mm headroom, 4 thumbscrews. Slope along each path averages about 3–4°, peaks at the apex and is zero at the ends, which is why the lead-ins exist.
- **Exits** step down to the return green (1.0 × 2.6 m at +67, cup 1.2 m out on the Express line) on carpeted 1:4 bevels, with no raw steps.

**Height check.** The review said "+190 if needed", but its drops don't fit under +190:

| Point | Height (mm) |
|---|---|
| Ramp crest | about +245 |
| Portal sills (0.5 m at 1:12) | about +203 |
| Outer loop entry (25 mm lead-in) | about +178 |
| Outer exit (5° across 1.16 m) | about +77 |
| Return green (10 mm carpeted step) | +67 |

So the target crest is **about +245 mm**: a 1:6 ramp about 1.47 m long, with the flat cut from 1.0 to 0.8 m to keep the U at 4.0 m. With slope drag that is about 2.3 m/s at the ramp foot and about 2.5 m/s from the adult tee, like hole 1. That breaks the review's own "at most about 1.8 m/s for a 6-year-old" (S1), so the junior tee at the ramp foot plus "after two roll-backs, place it on the platform for +1" carries small kids. If the ramp proves too hard, shorten the falling platform first; keep the cross-fall at 5° or more.

**Build.** Ramp and platform, loop box (about 1.3 × 0.75 m) with a plane floor and shaped lead-ins, arcs (carpenter cuts the master, CNC or volunteers the rest), lid, return green, a 0.4 m island box. The existing tile-wall sheet is re-cut with 3 portals. Carpenter 2.25 days (1.5). Volunteers about 1.5 days: white tile, invented line symbols (star, ring, diamond), a 1.6 m kiosk with battery globe lamps, ice-blue LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** Too soft rolls back down the ramp; every channel falls; the lid lifts off in under 20 s. Risks: loop stall is still the course's main physics unknown (the lever is a bigger entry drop, not a bigger radius); polycarbonate fire rating; the gap script needs a lidded-channel exemption. Hole 2's queue watches the exits. **11:59:** the platform clock.

### 2 Grand Central Whispering Gallery

**Plays.** Tee at the brass whisper spot. A round information booth blocks the straight line to the cup at the other spot, under the big clock. The sign: "Aim anywhere off the wall. Every bounce is the same distance." The game is the weight.

**Mechanism.** Ellipse a = 1.40, b = 0.95 m, so c = 1.03 m: tee at focus A, cup at focus B, every A-wall-B path 2.80 m. Booth Ø0.40 m, 0.35 m high, EVA-faced, cone top with four clock faces, 20 kg ballast, bolted; it blocks shots within about ±12° of the straight line. About 1.9–2.4 m/s from A arrives at B at 0.3–1.2 m/s. The whole ellipse is a +75 deck levelled to ±2 mm.

**Rail face (M7).** With 3 mm EVA and 25% cushion loss, a side-wall shot leaves about 8° off, about 0.3 m wide at B against a ±80 mm pass mark, and thicker EVA makes it worse. So: the three-lamination flexi-ply rail gets a **hard, low-friction lacquered ply or hardboard face, no EVA**; the tuning lever becomes harder and slicker; the 3 Dec rig uses the real face; the octagon fallback (3.0 m, 22.5° rails, same booth) is drawn and priced now. If scatter still fails, a shallow 1.5–2° dish centred on B, outside a 300 mm flat round the cup, lets gravity correct the error (+0.5 day).

**Build.** String-and-pins setting out, deck in three pieces (S8), laminated rail, booth, the existing Ø1 m clock at 11:59 on a 30 kg braced post behind B. Carpenter 1.75 days (1.25), +0.5 with the dish. Volunteers about 1 day: teal star field, brass booth and spots, amber LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** The ellipse returns every ball; nothing is more than 0.95 m from the rail. Risks: real rebounds (the rig decides), deck level, laminations opening. Its clock faces hole 1's 2027. **11:59:** the big clock.

### 1 Times Square

**Plays.** The finale. Putt up the red steps; too soft rolls back for another try. Over the crest the ball rattles down a lit peg board under a clear cover into a slot marked 3, 2, 1, 2, 3; the centre is DROP and the 2027 neon flashes. Score is roll-backs plus the slot number. Every ball rolls out to a tray and goes back to the hub.

**Mechanism (M3).** Lane 1.0 m. The **adult tee moves 0.6 m forward**, leaving 0.8 m of flat. Ramp 1:6, 1.2 m, to +200 with R 600 transitions: with slope drag about 2.06 m/s at the foot and about 2.3 m/s from the new tee; junior tee at the foot. The 0.3 m **crest tilts 3° toward the board**, narrowing to 0.80 m, so every ball that crests is in play. Board 0.80 × 0.80 m at 12°; 10 mm hardwood pegs 45 mm proud at 71 × 62 mm pitch, 11 rows, about 115 pegs; wall teeth keep every gap at 61 mm; 4 mm polycarbonate cover 55 mm up. Five 130 mm slots.

**Tray (M3).** The board's slot end sits only about 35 mm off the floor, so the internal trough couldn't be built. The slot lanes now run out through the front edge onto an open carpet-lined floor tray: about 25 mm drop, rails at least 90 mm, open lip on the walkway side, dividers extended 150 mm so everyone sees the slot. (A hidden trough would need a +260 crest and a 0.70 m board.) Expected score about 2.3–2.5.

**Build.** Ribbed ramp with flexi skin, jig-drilled board, cover, slots and tray; tower framed to about 2.8 m with 2027 neon, a borrowed mirror ball and an optional battery spinner (the only moving part, decor only); two billboards facing the party floor. Carpenter 2.25 days (1.75). Volunteers about 2 days: pegs, steps, invented billboard brands, magenta LED.

**Miss, risks, blends.** A weak putt rolls back; nothing can stop on the covered board or tilted crest; no ball is kept. Risks: kids cresting (junior tee, place rule); peg jams (200-drop rig); tower ballast and the microswitch (S9); fire rating; rolling aside if golf closes before midnight. The whole-room weenie. **11:59:** a billboard. Say "countdown ball", never the trademarked name.

## Course checks

- **Ten mechanics:** terraces, curling, cup-in-prop funnel, rally hill, banked wave, curved-rail Z, chaos field, side-swapping tubes, elliptical bank, plinko. One enclosed-channel hole (3); A and B each had two.
- **Moving parts:** one, the optional spinner on hole 1. The optional DROP microswitch would be a second; use the review's IR beam-break or nothing (S9).
