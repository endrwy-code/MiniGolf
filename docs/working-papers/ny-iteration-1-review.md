# New York iteration 1: the adversarial review (working paper, 27 Nov)

**Status:** the reviewer's full findings on the synthesised design. All must-fix items were folded into `../14-new-york-iteration-1.md` (its section "What the review changed" lists them as M1–M9); the should-fix items are open there as S1–S12. Must-fix 1–9 here are doc 14's M1–M9, and should-fix 1–12 are S1–S12, in the same order. Doc 14's cost comparison was later corrected (route C like for like is S$9.8k after the same cuts).

## Verdict

The course structure is worth committing to, but not as drawn. That covers the two-district hub and loops, the ten distinct mechanics, the shared-scenery blending, keeping the slot colours and signs, the light-pool 'rooms' and choosing hole 3 as the prototype. It delivers nearly every user wish (terraces, curling, an integrated cup, the rally hill, the wave, curves, left-in-right-out tubes, plinko) with a sound robustness rulebook and clean trademark choices.

Several hole numbers are physically wrong, though, and they are the ones the pace and schedule rest on:
- Hole 3: the planar loop floor gives about 3 deg with zero pull at every entry, not 5.8 deg.
- Hole 1: the plinko board ends about 35 mm off the floor, with no room for the trough.
- Hole 6: the wave ride lasts about 0.75 s against a 1.3 m face, so balls hit the cheek and come back.
- Hole 7: off-line balls cross the 0.45 m plateau above about 0.73 m/s, not 1.5.
- Hole 9: the backstop makes smashing the best strategy.
- Hole 2: the ellipse focus fails with an EVA face, and 'thicker EVA' makes it worse.

As drawn, realistic pace is about 3.0 min at the slowest holes, which gives about 60 to 65 players/h. The build is about 19 carpenter-days against 14.35 claimed, at about S$13 to 14.5k.

Recommendation:
1. This weekend, fix the hole 3 floor and platform before cutting starts on Mon 30 Nov.
2. Redraw 1, 6, 7 and 9 and the facade bracing before the Sat 5 Dec timber order.
3. Replace the bench rigs with full-width or real-part tests.
4. Make the ellipse-versus-fallback call on a test that uses the real rail face.
5. Book a second carpenter for about 6 to 8 days plus CNC cutting now.

With those fixes the slowest hole should come in at about 2.5 min (about 75 to 80 players/h). One-district rounds stay the peak plan.

## Must fix

### 1. Hole 3 The Subway: The loop floor as drawn cannot do what the numbers say. A single flat ply wedge can only fall across the box. It cannot fall along each hairpin. Each loop's drop is therefore proportional to its width (2r), not the full 95 mm. With a planar wedge falling 95 mm over the outer loop's 1.16 m span: the inner loop falls about 49 mm, the middle about 72 mm and the outer 95 mm. All three average about 3.0 deg along the path, not the claimed 5.8/3.9/3.0 deg. The slope along the path is about 0 at every entry and exit (the path runs across the fall line there) and peaks at only about 4.7 deg at the apex. The geometry also puts the three portal floors at about +162, +149 and +134 mm against a flat +162 platform, giving 13 and 28 mm down-steps. The exits land at about +90, +78 and +67 onto a +67 green, giving 23 and 11 mm steps.

**Why it fails:** The prototype is being cut on Mon 30 Nov from this spec. A dead-slow ball enters each hairpin exactly where the pull is zero and the wall pushes it inward. On painted ply (about 0.1 to 0.2 m/s² drag) it stalls in the first 50 to 100 mm under the tile wall. That is pass criterion 1, failed by geometry rather than by the physics unknown the prototype was meant to answer. The listed levers do not fix it: opening r1 to 0.35 m adds no fall, and a higher platform still gives zero pull at the entry. The steps the carpenter will improvise at the portals and exits are uncontrolled.

**Fix:** Before the carpenter starts, redraw the box this way. (1) Plane falling across the box only, at about 5 to 6 deg (platform +190 if needed). (2) Every portal threshold gets a deliberate 1:4 lead-in that drops at least 20 to 25 mm in the first 100 mm of channel, so every loop starts at 0.3 m/s or more whatever the arrival speed. (3) Exit floors step down to the return green on purpose with 1:4 bevels, carpeted, with no raw steps. (4) Correct the doc to 'about 3 deg average, 4.7 deg max, zero at the ends'. (5) Add to pass criterion 1 '30/30 dead-slow balls into the INNER loop placed at the portal and released at rest'. If it still stalls, the lever is a bigger entry drop, not a bigger radius.

### 2. Hole 3 The Subway: There is a pace trap between the crest and the portals. After the ramp there is a 0.8 m FLAT platform. Climbing the ramp with drag (+162 over 0.97 m) needs about 1.85 m/s at the foot. Reaching the portals as well needs about 1.0 m/s at the crest, so about 2.1 m/s at the foot and about 2.4 m/s from the adult tee (the doc says 2.0). Balls that just crest stop on the platform. About 35% of on-line balls hit a 30 mm portal nose (2 x (30 + 42.7) mm of the 420 mm mouth) and, as drawn, roll all the way back down the ramp.

**Why it fails:** In realistic family play this is about 1.8 strokes to get into a portal plus about 1.3 to putt out, so about 3.1 strokes each. 4 x 3.1 x (10 s + 3 s transit) plus about 25 s walking the U comes to about 2.8 to 3.1 min. That is over the 2.5 min ceiling on the one hole the whole schedule hangs on.

**Fix:** Make the platform 0.5 m long and fall 1:12 to 1:10 toward the portals. Any ball that crests then reaches a portal, and a nose bounce rolls back uphill a little and returns into a channel by itself instead of down the ramp. This is the same change as the entry lead-in in the previous item. Recompute the ramp numbers with slope drag included and move the junior tee to the ramp foot, where about 1.85 m/s is needed. Keep the 'two roll-backs, place it on the platform for +1' rule.

### 3. Hole 1 Times Square: The board-and-trough geometry does not close. The crest is at +200 mm. A 0.80 m board at 12 deg falls about 166 mm, so the slot end of the board sits at about +35 mm. There is no room under it for 'an internal trough falling 3 deg to a front pick-up tray'. That needs roughly ball 43 + board stack 24 + trough floor 9 + fall about 20 to 40 mm, so 95 to 115 mm below the slot floor. Also, the 0.3 m crest plateau is flat and open. A ball that crests at under about 0.6 m/s stops there, and no rule covers it. The ramp speed omits slope drag: about 2.06 m/s at the foot (doc 1.9) and about 2.5 m/s from the adult tee (doc 2.2).

**Why it fails:** The carpenter cannot build what is drawn. Improvising on site will produce either a lip the balls jam behind or a trough that does not drain, which needs a marshal at the finale. Balls parked on the crest stall the hole and cause scoring arguments. Kids who need about 2.1 m/s from the junior tee will roll back repeatedly.

**Fix:** Drop the internal trough. Run the five slot lanes out through the front edge onto an open, carpet-lined floor tray in front of them: a drop of about 25 mm, rails at least 90 mm, an open lip on the walkway side, and the dividers extended 150 mm so everyone can see which slot was hit. If a hidden trough is wanted, the alternative is crest +260 mm with a 0.70 m board, which costs about 0.25 m/s more putt. Tilt the crest plateau 3 deg toward the board so every ball that crests is in play. Move the adult tee 0.6 m forward.

### 4. Hole 6 Liberty Island: The 45 deg wave turn does not work in a 1.3 m face. A rolling ball in an R 450 transition behaves like a pendulum. Its time up the wave is about half a period, π√(7(R-r)/5g), about 0.75 s, almost the same at any speed. In that time the along-wave component (v/√2) carries it about 1.0 m at 2 m/s and about 1.6 m at 3 m/s. A ball on the lane centre meets the 45 deg face with only about 0.65 m of face ahead, and the downstream cheek is closed. Most putts therefore hit the cheek while they are up the wave. The rebound is about (-1-e, 1-e), so the ball comes back down leg A toward the player instead of turning 90 deg onto the island.

**Why it fails:** The 'wow' fails for the majority of putts, which become extra strokes. Pessimistically that is about 3.2 strokes each, so about 2.8 to 3.0 min, the slowest hole on the course. The planned bench rig (d), one rib with a 0.4 m skin, physically cannot show a cheek strike or along-wave travel. So the Sat 5 Dec wave-or-chamfer decision would be made on a test that cannot fail.

**Fix:** Replace rig (d) with a full-width section: a 45 deg face over the whole 1.84 m corner diagonal, real cheeks and a 3 m run-up. Take 20 putts at each of 1.5, 2 and 3 m/s. Pass: at least 70% leave toward the island. Design options, best first: (a) a conical banked corner, with the face curved in plan at about R 1.0 m on tapered radial ribs, skinned with 2 x 4 mm flexi-ply (a cone is a developable surface, so the ply wraps it). The ball rides round the bend at any speed (about +0.5 day). (b) Lengthen the 45 deg face to the full 1.84 m diagonal and drop to R 300 / 300 mm. This only partly helps. (c) The flat 45 deg chamfer bank (the existing fallback), with a painted breaking-wave cut-out rising behind it for the visual. Decide by Sat 5 Dec and hold the 'cup 0.3 m nearer the crest' pace lever in reserve.

### 5. Hole 7 Brooklyn Bridge: The plateau capture numbers are wrong. The plateau is 0.45 m and the lane 0.9 m. A ball that misses the Ø190 catch ring (offset over about 0.12 m, about 74% of the lane width) stops on the plateau only if it crests below √(2 x 0.6 x 0.45), about 0.73 m/s. Above that it runs over and down the other side. The doc says that only happens over 1.5 m/s. At the foot, the stop-on-top window is about 1.80 to 1.94 m/s (about ±4%), and from the tee about 2.19 to 2.31 m/s (about ±3%). A 2 deg cross-fall moves a climbing ball only about 3 cm sideways.

**Why it fails:** Almost every non-ace either rolls back or crosses to the other borough. Then players swap ends (a 6 m walk round the rails), get confused about whose ball is whose, and play their next shot from among the other pair. Realistically about 3.3 strokes each, two duels plus changeover plus swaps, so about 2.6 to 2.9 min. The doc's 1.8 min and its 'fastest format' pace protection depend on the wrong number.

**Fix:** (1) Make the bridge-deck rails converge from 0.9 m to 0.50 m over each ramp ('the roadway narrows between the towers'), so the ring covers about 40% of the line and the narrowing does the steering. (2) Widen the plateau to 0.60 m, which raises the stop speed to about 0.85 m/s. (3) Raise the cross-fall to 3 deg. (4) Rule: 'a ball that crosses is played by its owner from the end it reached, after the other player'. (5) Build the real hill (both ramps, the plateau and the ring) as the 3 Dec bench rig instead of a 0.6 m one-sided sample, and time a group of 4 on it.

### 6. Hole 9 Brooklyn Pizza: The scoring rewards smashing. A short ball fails the crust and scores 4. A modest overhit stops on the 4 deg run-out and scores 4 ('anywhere else'). A smash is different. At 3 m/s from the tee it reaches the EVA backstop at about 1.35 m/s, rebounds at about 0.5 to 0.7 m/s, runs down the 4 deg run-out and rolls about 0.3 m back onto the pizza, scoring 3. On the 4 deg run-out the pull (about 0.49 m/s²) is roughly equal to the drag (about 0.55, and less on needle-punch), so balls parked there creep back onto the crust ring too.

**Why it fails:** The scoring rises and falls with pace instead of getting steadily worse, so 'hit it hard' is the best strategy for every kid. That kills the weight skill the hole exists for. It also produces the hardest knock-on hits into other people's balls, and the dry run will show it within ten minutes.

**Fix:** Make 'long' final and the worst result. Replace the run-out and foam with a full-width 'oven' gutter behind the crust ring: 120 mm wide and 45 mm deep, its floor falling 3 deg to a 250 mm open pocket at one side rail for a hand pick-up. Any ball in the oven scores 4 and cannot come back. A cheaper alternative is a flat run-out with a dead backstop (carpet flap over open-cell foam) and the rule 'off the pizza = 4, even if it comes back'. The gutter is cleaner. Also correct the crust-foot speed: about 1.2 m/s with drag, not 1.0.

### 7. Hole 2 Grand Central Whispering Gallery: The focus-to-focus trick needs mirror-like rebounds, and a cushioned rail cannot give them. Take a shot from A sideways to the side wall at (-c, b²/a). It meets the wall at about 36 deg. With the design's own 25% cushion loss (normal restitution about 0.75) and the tangential speed kept, it leaves at about 44 deg, an error of about 8 deg over the 2.16 m to B, which is about 0.3 m. Friction against EVA can swing the error the other way by 10 to 20 deg. The rolling ball's topspin then curves the path after the bounce. Against a ±80 mm pass mark this almost certainly fails. Worse, the plan's first tuning lever, 'thicken the EVA to 5 mm', lowers restitution and makes the error larger. The 3 Dec rig is a hardboard ellipse, so it does not even test the face that will be built.

**Why it fails:** If the rig passes on hardboard and the hole is built with EVA, the sign 'every bounce finds the cup' is false on the night. Players aim off the wall as told and miss by 0.3 m every time. The result is a luck hole with a blocker in the middle, slow and frustrating. If the rig fails, the octagon has to be drawn and bought in a single day (Sat 5 Dec) with no prep.

**Fix:** (1) Test with the actual rail face, and with a hard low-friction face as the candidate: painted or lacquered ply or hardboard, no EVA. Hard faces get closest to mirror rebounds. (2) Reverse the tuning lever: harder and slicker, never thicker foam. (3) Draw the fallback in full now and price the timber for both, so Sat 5 Dec is a choice and not a redesign. (4) If keeping the 'whispering' story matters, the robust version is the ellipse with a shallow 1.5 to 2 deg dish centred on B, outside a 300 mm flat zone round the cup. Gravity then corrects the rebound error. It costs about +0.5 day (graded ribs plus a flexi skin, the same detail as the hill).

### 8. Hole 10 and 9 (shared brownstone facade): A 4.8 x 2.4 m facade (4 sheets of 9 mm on a frame) stands at z 9.6 to 9.9. That leaves exactly 1.75 m to the runway edge at z 11.65, which is the minimum (0.515 m speaker stands + 1.2 m walkway). The gap to hole 10's rail is 0.3 m and to hole 9's house about 0.15 m. A 2.4 m flat needs about 0.6 to 0.8 m of raking brace or jack footprint, and nothing in the design says how it is braced or weighted. The D6 rule calls for bracing over 2 m and at least 30 kg.

**Why it fails:** If braced from behind, the braces cut the runway-side walkway to about 1.0 m and fail the layout check. There is no room in front. Left unbraced, a 2.4 m sail that crowds lean on beside a walkway full of kids is a topple hazard, and the safety walk on 30 Dec will stop it.

**Fix:** Use the hole bodies as ballast. Steel angle outriggers run from the facade's base rail under hole 9's house frame and hole 10's box frames, screwed into them. One ballasted L-jack (2 x 15 kg) on the hole side covers the 2.2 m gap between the holes. Alternatively, build it as two hinged panels set in a shallow zig-zag. Either way, keep everything inside z 9.9 and rerun make_layout_nyc.py. Finish or drape the runway-facing back, because the stage audience sees it.

### 9. Hole Course (build schedule): The carpenter days are understated and the second-carpenter fix is too small. By the design's own count, 7 to 18 Dec needs about 11.6 carpenter-days in about 11 days, and in practice there are about 10 build days, because the 18th is the dry run. Realistic prop days for one-off curved and precision work: 10 = 1.25, 9 = 0.75, 8 = 1.0, 7 = 1.0, 6 = 2.0, 5 = 1.0, 4 = 0.5, 3 = 2.25, 2 = 1.75, 1 = 2.25. That is about 13.75, against 10.35 claimed and 8.0 for route C. Six bench rigs in 0.75 day is also optimistic; about 1.5 days is realistic.

**Why it fails:** About 11.5 prop days plus about 2.5 days of rails and greens (stepped, L, U, keyhole and curved frames), the booth and the jigs come to about 14.5 days of work in about 10 days. A mate for 3 days leaves about 1.5 to 2 holes unbuilt on Fri 18 Dec. Those holes then miss the dry run, which is the only timed test of pace.

**Fix:** Now, not on 7 Dec: book a second carpenter for about 6 to 8 days (7 to 18 Dec) for the box work (10, 9, 4, 8, rails and greens). Send CNC work out to a local router shop, cut from DXFs exported by make_carpenter_pack.py: all stacked arcs (3, 5), the hill, wave and ramp ribs, the ellipse deck and rail blocks, and the drilled plinko board and peg jig. That costs about S$400 to 800 and saves about 2 to 3 carpenter-days while hitting the ±1 mm tolerances the subway and plinko need. Make the bench rigs real parts (7's hill, one real corner of 5, a full-width wave section) so rig time isn't thrown away.

## Should fix

1. **Hole All ramp and bevel holes (3, 1, 7, 6, 8, 9, 4, 5): Every ramp threshold leaves out drag on the slope. The real speeds needed are about 12 to 20% higher: hole 1 needs 2.06 m/s at the foot (doc 1.9), hole 3 needs 1.85 just to crest (doc 1.7), and the 1:5 bevels need about 1.2 m/s (doc 1.0 to 1.05). The junior tees were placed using the low numbers. The toe detail where the 2 x 6 mm flexi skin of a hill or ramp meets a floor-laid lane is also not specified. Butted as drawn, the ball meets a 10 to 12 mm up-step at the foot.** Fix: Recompute every threshold as v² = 10gH/7 + 2·a·L(slope). Move junior tees so a 6-year-old needs at most about 1.8 m/s. Detail every ramp and hill toe as a feathered hardwood or aluminium tapered strip with the carpet running continuously over it (2 mm step at most). The route C bevel detail probably covers 4, 5, 8 and 9 already; add it to 7, 3, 1 and the wave.

2. **Hole 10 Brownstone Stoops: Dropping off a step does not add forward speed. The height is lost as vertical speed in the landing bounce, plus a little skid. The ace needs about 2.4 to 2.5 m/s, not 2.0 to 2.3. Players will also step onto the terraces to retrieve balls, whatever the sign says, and the 65/20/40 mm in-lane steps are backward-walking trip edges.** Fix: Correct the speed note. Specify the boxes to be stood on (joists at 400 centres or less, 150 kg point load). Glow tape on the top edge of every nosing, not only on the box ends. Allow 'step in to putt' instead of a rule nobody will follow.

3. **Hole 8 Hot Dog Cart: Each bun half fills from the rail with a 0.2 m flat mouth face square to the lane, which forms a 90 deg inside corner with the side rail. The 'funnel forgives ±0.3 m' claim is optimistic: converging 13 deg walls add 26 deg of cross angle per bounce, so off-line balls zig-zag across the 0.40 m channel and cross the cup more or less at random.** Fix: Put 45 deg fills in the two mouth-face/rail corners so mouth misses roll back to the kerb. Treat the ace rate as a dry-run number and keep the existing lever of moving the cup 0.1 m off axis.

4. **Hole 7 Brooklyn Bridge (towers): A 1.2 m high clear opening over the lane is at child head and adult chest height, exactly where people lean in to pick balls out of the plateau cup or reach over the rail.** Fix: Stand the towers over the flats, at least 0.6 m clear of the ramp foot, with the crossbeam at 2.1 m or more. Or make them open pylons with no crossbeam, keeping the festoon 'cables' at 2.4 m or more.

5. **Hole 6 Liberty Island (wave safety): A 400 mm curved ply wave with a curl lip next to the hub works as a skate ramp for kids: running up it, falls, and a 2 x 6 mm skin on ribs at 250 mm denting or cracking underfoot. This breaks the 'nothing climbable' rule.** Fix: Ribs at 150 mm or less, a 2 x 9 mm skin, a closed back, and a padded or rounded curl nosing. A 'no climbing' pictogram on the totem. Hole 6 already has a floating marshal, so brief them on it.

6. **Hole 5 Central Park Loop: A ball running along a wall slides against it at full speed until it picks up spin about the vertical axis. That costs up to about 29% of its speed per contact, and high-friction EVA makes it worse, not the 10 to 15% assumed. At 25 to 30% loss per corner, an ace needs about 3.3 m/s or more, so 'aces possible on all ten' is doubtful here. Pace is realistically about 2.5 to 2.7 min. The two borrowed Christmas trees (base about 1.2 to 1.8 m) 'outside the outer corners' fit the Z's inside pockets only if they are slim. Otherwise they eat the 2.0 m gap to hole 2 and the 1.75 m runway clearance.** Fix: Face the curves with a hard, low-friction finish (lacquered ply or a PVC strip), not EVA. Measure the loss on rig (e) with that face. If the loss is over 20%, move the bevel 0.3 m nearer the cup. Specify trees with a base of 0.9 m or less (or festoon arches), placed inside the Z's pockets, and rerun the clearance check.

7. **Hole 4 Gridlock: The two cabs set at 30 deg 'closed to the rail' form acute V pockets between the cab face and the rail. The gap check passes them (gap 0), but a ball wedges where the putter cannot get behind it. Chaos fields also run long: about 3.0 to 3.2 family strokes, so about 2.5 to 2.6 min.** Fix: Fill each acute V with a triangular block screwed down, or set the angled cabs at least 0.27 m clear at both ends. Add an acute-angle test to the pack gap script. Keep the 'open one avenue' lever ready.

8. **Hole 2 Grand Central (build): The ellipse deck is 2.8 m long but the sheets are 2.44 m, so it cannot be '2 sheets'. There will be a seam across a green that needs ±2 mm.** Fix: Use three pieces with the seams kept more than 300 mm from B and from the tee spot, biscuited, sanded flush and roll-tested.

9. **Hole 1 Times Square (tower and switch): The 2.8 m tower at the party-floor edge exceeds the D6 '30 kg up to 2.5 m' rule. The DROP microswitch is a mechanical moving part on a course allowed only one, and the spinner is already that one.** Fix: Brace the tower to the hole frame and ballast it with at least 60 kg. Use an IR beam-break in the DROP slot instead of a microswitch, so the effect has no moving parts.

10. **Hole Course (lighting): In route C the hole-colour strip sits in a rebate on the OUTSIDE bottom edge of the rail. It lights the floor outside the rails, not the cup end. On the long holes (1, 6, 9, 10) the green would be lit only by the 50 to 100 lux house wash, below the doc's own 200 lux target.** Fix: Aim the totem flood to cover the cup end, or add a second small 24 V flood on the pun-sign posts for holes over about 4 m. Measure at the dry run.

11. **Hole Course (theme wording): 'Stand Clear of the Closing Doors' is the subway's real announcement wording and appears on transit-licensed merchandise. 'Grand Central' is also a licensed terminal name. The risk is low at a private event, but photos will go online.** Fix: Use a pun twist that is funnier anyway, for example 'Stand Clear of the Closing Year'. Keep the booth and clock generic, without copying the real four-faced opal clock exactly.

12. **Hole 9 Brooklyn Pizza (surface): A needle-punch inlay with taped seams under dying curling balls (0.1 to 0.4 m/s) deflects them at every 1 mm ridge, and it costs 1.5 volunteer-days in the busiest weeks.** Fix: Lay the house in one carpet and paint or print the pizza and pepperoni. That gives even speed with no seams and saves about 1 volunteer-day.

## Pace audit

These are pessimistic minutes for an excited family group of 4 at 11 pm, including retrieval, walking and scoring. The design's own figure is in brackets.

- 10 Stoops: 2.3 (2.0). About 2.7 strokes each, walking a 5.4 m lane and retrieving from terraces.
- 9 Pizza: 1.9 to 2.1 (1.7). Each shot waits for the ball to stop, scoring disputes take time, and kids ask for another go.
- 8 Hot Dog: 2.1 to 2.3 (2.0). Some retrievals from the channel.
- 7 Bridge as drawn: 2.6 to 2.9 (1.8). FLAG: off-line balls cross over above about 0.73 m/s, so players swap ends; about 3.3 strokes; pair changeover. After the converging-rail fix, about 2.2 to 2.4.
- 6 Liberty as drawn: 2.8 to 3.1 (2.3). FLAG: cheek strikes return the ball down leg A; about 3.2 strokes; walking the L. After the banked-corner or chamfer fix, about 2.4.
- 5 Central Park: 2.5 to 2.7 (2.3). FLAG, borderline: corner scrub means three legs usually take three putts.
- 4 Gridlock: 2.5 to 2.6 (2.3). FLAG, borderline: chaos strokes.
- 3 Subway as drawn: 2.8 to 3.1 (2.3). FLAG: balls stop on the flat platform, nose bounces roll back down the ramp, about 3.1 strokes, plus walking the U. After the falling-platform fix, about 2.3 to 2.5.
- 2 Grand Central: 2.2 to 2.4 if the rebounds are close to mirror-like, 2.5 to 2.7 if not (likely with EVA). FLAG if not.
- 1 Times Square: 2.2 to 2.5 (2.0). About 1.6 to 1.8 ramp attempts at the real 2.5 m/s, rattle time, tray pick-up and cheering.

Course as drawn: the slowest holes (6 and 3) are at about 3.0 min, which gives about 20 groups/h, about 70 players/h at 3.5 per group and about 60 to 65 after stochastic loss. That is under the 77 to 90 target.

With the must-fixes on 3, 6, 7 and 9 applied, the slowest is about 2.5 min: about 24 groups/h, about 84 nominal, about 75 to 80 real. That is at the bottom of the target, so plan for one-district rounds at peak from the start.

Every number must be replaced by dry-run timings with a kids' group on 18 Dec. Run tools/flow_sim.py now with these pessimistic times, not the design's.
