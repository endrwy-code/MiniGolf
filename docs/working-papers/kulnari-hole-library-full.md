### K01 Furnace Room (Kulnari hole 1 (user anchor; high))

**Kulnari room or zone:** Boiler/furnace basement. Possibly the venue's 'Engine Room' (that name appears only in an unverified TikTok search summary, so this is inference).

**Sources:**

- Phone A IMG_2897 0-28 s
- Phone A IMG_2898 0-38 s
- Phone A IMG_2899 0-24 s
- Not on phone B (B starts at hole 2)

**Room and vibe:** A cramped, dark, low-ceiling industrial cell. There is a brick furnace with a metal firebox grate (red LED at the grate) and a round boiler drum. Brass shell-casing and pipe props stand around it. Walls are distressed wooden doors and metal industrial doors. The hole sign sits on a red wooden lectern under blue light. One corner is red-lit, and an orange caged bulkhead lamp hangs over the second chamber. It feels like a spooky basement.

**Layout:** Two stages, split by a wall or doorway. Stage 1 (tee): a beige carpet strip about 25-30 cm wide and 2-2.5 m long. Two flat stained battens converge on it in a V toward the furnace firebox. Stage 2 is through a doorway: a dark grey carpet pad about 1.2 x 2 m with chamfered corners and a pale timber kerb about 10 cm high. When players walk round, the ball is already on this pad. In the far corner a raw timber plank about 25 cm wide and 1 m long rises as a ramp through white foam 'rubble' blocks to a small round metal pot by the wall.

**Gimmicks:**

- Furnace as a vanishing tunnel: the tee lane funnels into the firebox and the ball turns up in a separate chamber. Inferred from the ball being on the pad when players arrive; the passage itself was not seen.
- Plank ramp up through foam rubble to a raised pot. Pure weight control: too soft rolls back, too hard flies into the rubble.
- Hidden second chamber: the target is invisible from the tee.

**Construction:** Tee lane: a carpet offcut on a painted floor, edged by flat stained battens about 8 x 2 cm with chevron-cut ends (low enough to hop). Chamber 2: a grey needle-punch carpet pad inside a plain pine kerb about 10 cm high. Rubble: light white foam or plaster blocks with drawn circles. Ramp: an unfixed raw plank propped on blocks, with a thin red-edged plank as a side guide. Theming comes from the props (furnace front, boiler drum, shell casings), not from the lane.

**Cup:** Probably the small round metal pot or helmet-like vessel at the top of the ramp. A ball is picked out by hand there at IMG_2899 @10 s. Diameter and depth are unknown (medium-low confidence).

**Play notes:** In IMG_2898, tee putts come about 8 s apart (0-4, 12-16, 20-28 s), and the group walks to chamber 2 at about 30 s. In IMG_2899, ramp putts at about 2, 14 and 20 s, a pick-out at 10 s, and a celebration at 24 s. The whole hole took roughly 2-3 min for the group (estimate).

**What makes it good:** A 'where did my ball go?' reveal in the very first hole sets the mystery tone. The props are the obstacles, and the walk to a second room makes the hole feel like a short story.

**Weaknesses:** The 2 cm battens let the ball hop out. The ramp is loose and movable. It is very dark. Because the tunnel exit can't be seen, the player never watches the payoff.

**Lesson for our course:** Copy the 'ball vanishes into a prop and reappears elsewhere' idea, but in the open auditorium put the exit where the waiting group can see it. For New York, a subway-entrance box could drop the ball onto a lower 'platform' lane. Use rails of at least 5-8 cm, and fix any ramp with screws.

### K02 Vault Attic Idol (Kulnari hole 2 (user anchor; high))

**Kulnari room or zone:** An antiques/artefacts attic. It fits the venue's teased 'Vault Room' ('Time has stood still... The artefacts remain'), but that is inference.

**Sources:**

- Phone B a.MOV 0-57 s
- Phone B IMG_0014 0-22 s
- Not on phone A
- Viewed in this pass: dense sheets B01_a_p1 (0-34 s) and B02_IMG_0014_p1

**Room and vibe:** A small, low attic with sloped grey-blue ceiling planes and grey-blue plaster walls. Decor: a framed blue seascape and a framed map, Chinese calligraphy prints, a wall shelf, flame-effect sconces, wooden chests with a bronze urn, a black cat figure, and a glass-topped display chest of brass instruments with a porcelain skull. Dim, lit by the phone torch, with a green exit light.

**Layout:** A roughly straight lane, about 5-6 m long and 1.5-2 m wide, on the room's grey carpet. The tee is beside the line of wooden chests. Mid-lane is a dark box plinth (about 30 cm cube) with an idol figurine, and the ball has to go round it. A long, straight pale timber batten lies diagonally on the carpet as a rail. The cup is about 0.5 m from the back wall beside the chests and the display chest. No elevation change. A leaning board at the far wall may act as a backstop.

**Gimmicks:**

- Statue-on-plinth blocker in the direct line
- Furniture as rails: antique chests and a display case make the side walls, and their uneven faces give odd rebounds
- Room-is-the-hole: there are no built lane edges apart from one loose batten

**Construction:** About as cheap as it gets. The surface is the room's own grey needle-punch carpet (no deck, no turf). The cup is a hole in the carpet with a light rim or liner, about 10-11 cm. There is one loose batten of about 5 x 5 cm and one free-standing MDF/timber box with a figurine. All the theming comes from decor.

**Cup:** A plain hole in the grey carpet with a white or light rim, about 0.5 m from the back wall (IMG_0014 t0031; the white disc is visible near the player's feet at 14-16 s on the dense sheet). No prop is integrated.

**Play notes:** a.MOV: the red-ball player tees at 0-4 s, plays round the plinth at 12-20 s, putts by the chests and black cat at 22-36 s, and finishes at about 46-50 s (about 55 s). IMG_0014: the pink-ball player tees at 0-4 s and finishes at about 16 s (about 20 s). Putts come 4-8 s apart.

**What makes it good:** It costs almost nothing, and the room's story props carry the whole experience. The single blocker forces an angle shot without frustrating anyone.

**Weaknesses:** Very dark. The lane boundary is unclear, so the ball can wander among the furniture. Close to a 'nothing hole' in golf terms. No curved guide was seen in the footage (relevant to the user's memory).

**Lesson for our course:** Furniture and props can be rails if their faces are reasonably flat. Good for a New York 'brownstone parlour' or 'antique shop' hole using borrowed church furniture. Add one central blocker for the angle shot. In a bright auditorium, frame the lane clearly with a rug or tape so players know where it is.

### K03 Riveted Curve (photo-only) (Kulnari hole 3 (best estimate from the user's statement; medium-low). Alternative: these photos are part of hole 2.)

**Kulnari room or zone:** The same grey-carpet industrial/vault zone as holes 1-2. Riveted steel fits 'Engine Room' or 'Vault' (inference).

**Sources:**

- Photo P4 photo_2026-11-26_16-45-41.jpg
- Photo P5 photo_2026-11-26_16-45-43.jpg
- Not in any video clip from either phone

**Room and vibe:** Grey needle-punch carpet. Behind sits a dark riveted steel wall panel (rows of rivet heads at floor level). There is an olive-brown panelled wooden crate or chest at the upper left, and a pale grey wall or column on the right edge. Industrial and vault-like, the same family as hole 1's metal doors and hole 2's grey carpet and chests.

**Layout:** Only partly seen. A curved bank rail starts at the crate and sweeps through roughly a quarter to a third of a circle (radius about 0.8-1 m, estimated against a shoe) toward the side wall. It forms a rounded corner that turns the ball. A cup cut into the same kind of grey carpet (P5) is elsewhere, and its position relative to the curve is unknown.

**Gimmicks:**

- Curved guide or bank rail turning the ball round a corner. This is the only curved floor rail in the whole photo and video set, and the best match for the user's 'curves to guide the ball' memory.

**Construction:** The rail is about 4 thin timber laminations (a few mm each) bent to the arc and glued or nailed, roughly 3-5 cm tall (estimate). It is delaminating, and the free end is splintered with strips peeling off. Cup (P5): a round hole cut through a layered tan board (plywood or particleboard, with chipped and torn plies at the edge). Inside is a white PVC pipe sleeve over a rough cement bottom, deep enough to hold two balls completely below the surface. Carpet is glued on top.

**Cup:** P5: a deep sleeved cup in grey carpet holding a red and a pink ball. It is regulation-ish in width (about 105-110 mm against the 42.7 mm ball, estimated) and deeper than the ball stack.

**Play notes:** In P4 a player stands at the curve with a putter mid-hole. In P5 the red-ball and pink-ball players have both holed out. No timing data.

**What makes it good:** A smooth curve gives a predictable, satisfying bank shot on true carpet. The deep sleeved cup never spits the ball out.

**Weaknesses:** The bent lamination is failing at its free end (splinter risk, uneven rebound). The cup's cut edge exposes chipped board layers with no flange or liner lip. Dark.

**Lesson for our course:** Build curves from 2 layers of 6 mm bending ply (or flexible PVC trim), screwed to blocks every 200 mm. Cap or bury every free end. Fit a liner or PVC sleeve with a lip into every cup cut, and seal the board edge. Grey carpet is a cheap, true surface for a New York 'subway platform' or 'office floor' hole.

**Construction:** The quarter-pipe is a thin ply or veneered hardboard skin bent over curved ribs, with rows of screw heads visible (IMG_0017 t0045), stained and varnished to match the floor. The floor is the building's real floorboards, so no rails are needed. The turf island is cut free-form and laid on the wood with a white liner. Curtains close off the sides.

**Play notes:** The tee shot is at about 2-4 s on B (about 0-2 s on A). By A 6 s the pink ball already sits on the lower floor while the player walks down from the upper level. Phone B's 'next room with green turf, red rope and red curtains' is in fact this hole's own lower green. A hole-in-one is NOT supported. One player took about 25 s.

**What makes it good:** One big, legible feature (the ramp) carries the hole. The ball travels through two spaces, and the lower room lets watchers see the ball arrive. Fast wood plus a soft turf target gives a clear risk and reward.

**Weaknesses:** So dark that players needed torches. Curtains are soft boundaries the ball can roll under. The route needs a walk and stairs. The rebound line off the ramp is semi-random.

**Lesson for our course:** The best single 'wow' build to copy. Make a quarter-pipe from 6 mm ply over cut-ply ribs at about 300 mm centres, as a New York 'skate park', a 'Guggenheim spiral' or a 'Brooklyn Bridge' arc. In an open auditorium, use a low stage riser for the drop and add a kicker board under any drape.

## 8. Lessons across holes

1) Most of the theme is in the set dressing, not the lane. Most Kulnari lanes are plain carpet, turf or floorboards with basic edges. The feel comes from props, wall finishes (faux-brick panels, ivy and hedge mats, flower walls, fishnet 'tree trunks'), lighting and red lectern signs.
