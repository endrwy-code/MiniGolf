# Route C: one carpenter plus volunteers, about S$6–10k (draft v1, 18 Nov)

**Status:** Claude's breakdown of route C in `08-budget-model.md` section 6 ("pay a carpenter; volunteers paint and dress"), written on 18 Nov after the team asked how it could actually be done. It's a proposal, not a decision. Every price is an **estimate** from Singapore listings or the earlier docs, not a quote. Singapore retail prices include 9% GST.

## 1. The short answer

- **It's doable, but not as the full New York design.** The full design's materials alone come to about S$11–22k (`08-budget-model.md` section 8). Route C keeps the New York idea and builds it more simply: holes laid on the floor, flat cut-out props, one LED layer. What it keeps: the same ten places, the charcoal-and-white rail, each hole's own glow colour, a pun sign and a street-sign totem on every hole, the Tee Booth, and "find the 11:59".
- **Plan on about S$9.5k, including 10% contingency.** That leaves only about S$0.5k under a S$10k ceiling, so the cut list in section 6 matters. Getting down to about S$6.3k needs every line at its low end: production lends the lighting, a carpenter at about S$150–180 a day, borrowed putters and timber offcuts.
- **Where the money goes:** about a third on the carpenter and just over a quarter on rails, timber and surface. The rest is glow, paint, fixings, printing and kit.
- **What it depends on, in order:**
  1. A carpenter free for about 12 days between 30 Nov and 29 Dec.
  2. A workshop or spare hall to build in.
  3. A flat venue floor.
  4. Production agreeing to a dim warm wash, and lending RCD power boards (ideally lights too).
  5. About 45–50 volunteer-days, about 35 of them before 18 Dec.

## 2. What changes from the full New York design

| Part | Full design (`10-new-york-concept.md`) | Route C | What it saves, and what you give up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | 75 mm plywood decks on battens | Surface laid straight on the floor. One small raised **cup pad** per hole (about 0.8 × 0.8 m, 50–60 mm high, bevelled on the approach), so the cup can be sunk | Saves about 45 sheets of plywood, 280 m of battens and most of the carpentry. The holes look lower. The floor must be flat, and cables still can't cross under holes |
| Rails | 45 × 95 mm timber, charcoal with a white cap | **The same.** Each hole's rail is joined at every corner into one closed frame, held down by brackets on thin hardboard plates under the surface, so nothing sticks out into the walkway | Nothing. This is the one line not to cheapen: a rail that moves or flexes spoils every hole |
| Glow | Two LED layers, about 300 m | One layer, about 140 m, along the outside base of each rail in the hole's colour. It washes the floor and marks the edge | About S$0.5–1k. The playing edge isn't lit from under the cap, so the room wash matters more |
| Signs | Ten LED-neon pun signs | Ten painted pun signs; LED neon flex only on hole 1 and the Tee Booth | About S$300–600. Eight signs are lit by the hole's glow and the room, not self-lit |
| Props | Built 3D props (apple, torch arm, cart, water tower, clock booth, 3.6 m tower) | Flat, double-sided cut-outs on braced, weighted bases, plus found objects (bike wheels, a beach umbrella, cones, a borrowed tree and mirror ball) | Most of the prop carpentry. Cut-outs read well from the tee and in photos, less well from the side |
| Tallest | 3.6 m ball-drop tower | About 2.4–3 m, or production's truss if they can lend one | Less presence across the room |
| Skyline | Flats along both side walls (about 18 sheets) | Four flats: two behind the Tee Booth and one at the far end of each district. Ask production for a skyline on the stage screens while golf runs | About S$400–800 and many painting days |
| Surface | About 55 m² of putting turf, plus grey carpet and a white rink | 10 mm carpet grass (green), needle-punch carpet (charcoal, grey and blue), white felt or needle-punch for the rink | About S$1–1.5k. Carpet grass rolls well on a flat floor but looks less like a putting green. Upgrade in section 6 |
| Moving part | Rink turntable | None: the skaters are fixed bumpers (the full design's own fallback) | A motor, a battery and a failure risk |
| Effects | Cab roof light; tower lights up when the ball drops | Dropped | Small |
| Printing | About 45 m² | Ten totem panels and the scorecards; everything else painted by volunteers | About S$500–1,500 |

**The layout stays.** The hole footprints don't change, so layout v3 and its clearance checks still apply.

## 3. Hole by hole

Carpenter time covers the structure only. Volunteers paint and dress everything.

| # | Hole | How it's built in route C | Carpenter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | Bike Lane | Unchanged. Five free bike wheels cable-tied into timber cradles; the cradles screw through the surface into hardboard plates under it. Painted bike-lane stencil | 0.25 day (one jig; volunteers make the rest) |
| 9 | Hot Dog Cart | A flat cart front with an arch the ball runs under, screwed to the rails, and a striped beach umbrella clamped to it. The hot dog is a 1 m length of rail timber with rounded ends, painted as a sausage in a bun and laid as a bank wall. Fries are painted offcuts in a box; the bottles are painted cardboard tubes touching the rails | 0.5 day |
| 8 | The Big Apple | One flat apple about 1.5 m tall, double-sided (two thin sheets on a timber frame), with the boxed tunnel at its base (0.32 m wide, sloped floor, lift-off top). Leaf boards funnel a miss back. Braced and sandbagged behind, outside the rails | 1 day |
| 7 | Gridlock | As designed: the cabs are painted timber offcuts screwed through the charcoal carpet into hardboard plates. Ten small cones. No roof light | 0.25 day (a jig; volunteers fix the cabs) |
| 6 | Liberty Island | The island is a round cup pad (about 1.2 m), bevelled all round, reached by a 0.6 m gangway ramp. The harbour is blue carpet inside the rails: a miss stays in the harbour and is putted back up onto the island, so no marshal is needed. The torch arm is a flat cut-out (about 2.4 m) on a weighted base behind the island, with a battery LED flame | 1 day |
| 5 | The Subway | One 18 mm sheet on edge across the lane with three arched tunnels cut through it, braced to the rails. Volunteers paint it as white tile with grout lines and line circles. The car front is a painted thin sheet behind it | 0.75 day |
| 4 | **Broadway** (a swap; the team's call) | From the bench: putt through a red strip curtain onto a raised "stage" green (the cup pad) under a proscenium flat with festoon "marquee" bulbs. **Why swap:** Rooftop's ramp, raised green, guards and 2.4 m water tower are the most carpentry of any mid hole. Keeping Rooftop costs about S$300–500 and 1.5 carpenter-days more | 0.75 day |
| 3 | The Rink | White felt or needle-punch rink (test the speed at the prototype). The rail is painted white with a red stripe, as rink boards. Three skater cut-outs fixed on blocks as bumpers. The borrowed Christmas tree behind | 0.5 day |
| 2 | Grand Central | A 3 m clock green in carpet grass with an **eight-sided rail** (straight cuts, no bent plywood). Numerals cut from white felt; the hands are rail timber, screwed through the carpet into a hardboard disc under it. A painted 1 m clock face on a post outside the rails. The teal ceiling backdrop is two painted thin sheets | 0.75 day |
| 1 | Times Square | A timber hump under the surface, painted as the red steps. Two billboard flats on their own feet, lit by LED strip. A framed tower about 2.4–3 m tall, skinned in thin plywood, with "2027" in LED neon flex and a borrowed mirror ball on top (or production's truss). The cup is a mouth in the tower's base: the ball rolls in and drops into a box, so the hole keeps the balls | 1.5 days |
| Hub | Tee Booth | A red booth front on a timber frame around a borrowed table; "NEW YORK · 11:59" in LED neon flex; ball bins | 0.5 day |
| All | Totems ×10 | Painted 2 × 2 posts on weighted bases, with printed street blades and a subway-style bullet | 0.25 day (a jig; volunteers build) |
| All | Rails and cup pads | About 130 m of rail cut, jointed and fitted with brackets; ten cup pads | 2 days |
| | **Total** | About 10 days of building (the prototype included), plus 1 day at the dry run and 1 at the install | **≈ 12 days** |

"Find the 11:59" stays on every hole. It's painted or a sticker, so it costs nothing.

**Details the carpenter settles at the prototype (G2):**
- **Cup pad:** two layers of 18 mm plywood, or a batten frame with a 9 mm top; its height and bevel. The cup liner is a ring of 110 mm drain pipe with a felt disc under it, so the floor isn't marked.
- **Rails:** corners joined so each hole's rail is one closed frame; how many hidden plates it needs.
- **Step-in at the tee:** a lower, bevelled rail section at the tee end, so players don't step over a full rail in a dim room. Check that balls can't escape over it.
- **Surface:** carpet tape on the plates and along the rail line only; floor-safe tape at the edges.

**Reading the range.** Every line won't land high at once, but the spread is why the carpenter's quote and the timber price come before G1, and why section 6 has a cut list. If the carpenter quotes company rates (S$400 a day), this route doesn't fit S$10k without cutting days.

## 6. Holding S$10k: the cut list, and the upgrades

**If quotes come in high, cut in this order** (least visible first):

1. Ask production to lend lights (uplighters, or spare LED tape in the ten colours): up to S$650.
2. Use old set flats and offcuts from Christmas production for the props and flats: about S$200–400.
3. Volunteers build the totems, cradles, cabs and booth from the carpenter's jigs: 1–2 carpenter-days, about S$250–600.
4. Borrow all 40 putters: about S$150–250.
5. Two skyline flats instead of four, and no Grand Central backdrop (keep the clock): about S$150, plus several painting days.

**If money is left, add in this order:**

1. Putting turf instead of carpet grass on the green holes: about +S$1.1–1.5k. It rolls truer and looks like the references.
2. Keep Rooftop instead of Broadway: about +S$300–500 and 1.5 carpenter-days.
3. LED neon flex on all ten pun signs: about +S$250–450.
4. A second LED layer under the rail cap: about +S$500–900.

## 8. Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| No carpenter free in December | It's the peak month for event carpentry, and the whole route hangs on one person | Ask this week; book fixed days in writing; keep a second name. Fallbacks: route D with a handy volunteer as lead, or route A (rented carnival lanes) |
| The floor isn't flat, or is carpeted | Floor-laid holes take the floor's shape: a tilt or a soft floor spoils the roll | Check by Fri 20 Nov. Shim small dips under the cup pads and plates. A bad floor needs decks, which this budget can't cover |
| Rails slide or bounce | Spoils play | Test the hidden plates hard at the prototype. Add sandbags behind the rails where they stay out of the walkways |
| Production can't light the room or lend lights | The glow look needs a dim room you can still see in | Ask this week. The plan already assumes we buy the LED. Without a wash, keep the house lights low rather than off |
| Volunteer hours clash with Christmas | About 35 volunteer-days before 18 Dec, in the busiest weeks | Fix the paint nights now. Keep the paint scheme simple: one rail colour, two-colour props |
| Tape marks the floor | Venue damage | A 24-hour test on the real floor before buying |
| Budget creep | Only about S$0.5k between the plan and the ceiling | One person approves spending. Use the cut list before adding anything |
| Fire rating | Carpet, felt and paint in a public venue | Ask the venue what it needs; ask sellers for certificates |
