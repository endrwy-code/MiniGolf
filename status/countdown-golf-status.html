How many people get to play

Simulated with tools/flow_sim.py: a 3-hour session, a queue that never empties, groups of 4, hole times varying about ±35%, 300 runs per case. A model, not a measurement: time the real holes at the dry run.

Balanced holes (all 2.25 min) Uneven (hero holes 3.25 min, others 2.0)
players per hour
75–90players an hour to plan on for all 10 holes: about 230–270 people over 3 hours
≈ 2.3×as many if each group plays one 5-hole district when the queue is long
  • The ceiling is 100–120 an hour at 2–2.5 minutes a hole. The course never reaches it: it takes time to fill, and groups vary.
  • The slowest hole sets the pace. Four hero holes a minute slower than the rest cut capacity by about a quarter. Keep every hole within about 30 seconds of the others.
  • Story slows play. Kulnari, the indoor course with clues, runs about 3–7 minutes a hole by its listing. Keep "find the 11:59" something families do while they wait, and time it on the visit.
  • Capping the course at about 12 groups costs little and cuts rounds by 10–25 minutes. The waiting moves to the queue.

Budget ceiling: still not set. It was due at Gate 0 on Fri 20 Nov. It decides which of these rows is possible, so it's the first item for the decision meeting.
  • Route C is the cheapest route that keeps the New York look. About a quarter goes on the carpenter and over a third on rails, timber and surface. It needs a carpenter free for about 12 days, a workshop, a flat venue floor, a dim wash from production, and 45–50 volunteer-days.
  • The full New York design costs more in materials alone (≈ S$11–22k with the recommended China mix) than route C's whole plan. That's why route C lays the holes on the floor and uses flat cut-out props.
  • Renting can't stand in. Ten carnival lanes cost about S$1–5.5k a day before GST and delivery, but they are short, flat and unthemed.
  • GL025 tiles would add very roughly S$3–4k before freight inside route C (≈ S$4.5–5k for the tiles), and save some plywood and a carpenter-day. Only with a written delivery date of 10 Dec or earlier.
  • Don't cheapen the rails. Solid timber held still; foam, pool noodles and PVC pipe kill the rebound.

Safety flags raise now

  • Two circuits are over 100% on the cable plan: ND18 (LX4.2) at 101% and ND21 (LX6) at 106%. Breakers can trip mid-event. Raise it with whoever owns power, golf or not.
  • Trip hazards: cable runs cross both floor halves. Holes and queues must not add trip points; cable covers wherever a walkway crosses a run.
  • Course power: 12/24 V only, from a dedicated RCD-protected circuit, never ND18 or ND21. Production's electrician does anything at mains voltage.
  • Little slack: some clearances sit close to their rule (see the map), and every position is ±1 m until the stage depth is measured.
  • Exits: keep every door and its sign clear of holes, flats and queues.
